## Summary

This profile is a borderline inclusion. m-Path here delivers an intervention rather than collecting research data. It is included because the paper reports install, activation, day-level use and stage completion for the platform arm, which is the deployment reality a team reusing m-Path for an intervention would need.

The install step lost few people. 48 of 51 allocated participants (94.12%) installed the app. 43 (84.31%) interacted with it at least once.

Engagement then held through three five-day stages. Among the 48 who installed, 41 (85.41%) completed the first stage. 38 (79.16%) completed the second. 35 (72.92%) completed the third.

On average, participants used the app on 10.34 of the 15 days. On active days, they reported completing 68.15% of the proposed activities.

Outcome-questionnaire retention fell faster than app use. 42 of the nCompass arm completed post-test. 32 completed the two-week follow-up.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

Bring your own device. Participants allocated to nCompass got installation instructions at the end of the Qualtrics form. They signed up in m-Path with a code. The OS split is not reported.

An in-app message told users the programme would start the next day and asked them to enable push notifications. Users with technical difficulties were told to email the team and were helped by email or phone. How many asked for help is not reported.

Each day had three touchpoints. A morning notification introduced an emotional need and asked the user to schedule an activity. An afternoon reminder followed. An evening prompt, 12 hours after the morning interaction, asked whether the activity was done and collected ratings. Stage two added audio defusion exercises. Stage three added corrective messages. Reward badges were given for improvements.

## Recruitment and Retention

Recruitment ran through Romanian Facebook community groups from March to November 2023. The programme was advertised as free online self-help. Eligibility was screened inside the Qualtrics form. Ineligible respondents were emailed a list of helplines and free support services. The number screened is not given in the text.

Post-test and follow-up questionnaires were sent by email. The authors call follow-up attrition "relatively high." They note there was "no certain way to ensure their adherence at follow-up, considering the exclusively online communication."

The percentages printed for retention do not all reconcile with 51 per arm. The nCompass follow-up is given as 32 (62.45%). The control arm is given as 50 (94.33%) at post-test and 40 (75.47%) at follow-up. The paper does not explain the denominator.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

App engagement was measured as install, at least one interaction, completion of each stage, days of use and self-reported activity completion. Among the 43 active users, stage completion was 93.02%, 83.72% and 74.41%.

The paper does not report missing evening ratings, notification delivery, app errors or how many participants needed technical help.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude the nCompass programme was feasible and acceptable. They say retention was comparable to similar behavioural activation apps. Most participants rated it easy to use, useful and satisfying. For example, "The nCompass program was easy to use" scored a mean of 4.55 of 5.

They recommend more extensive strategies to incentivise completion of study measures in future fully online trials.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

m-Path can carry a multi-stage, three-times-a-day intervention with low setup loss. 94% installed and 84% became active with only written instructions and email support.

Plan for questionnaire attrition separately from app attrition. Here the app held 35 of 48 installers to the end of stage three, while only 32 of 51 returned the follow-up.

A lottery of five vouchers was the only incentive. That may explain part of the follow-up loss, and the authors point to incentives as the fix.

## Evidence Confidence

Reported. The install, activation, stage-completion and day-use figures come from a single published RCT report, read from the published PDF held by the Bilkent University repository on 2026-10-31. They are simple counts with no independent check.

Unclear. Several retention percentages do not reconcile with the stated arm sizes. The participant flow diagram (Figure 2) is an image and was not read. Funding is not stated.
