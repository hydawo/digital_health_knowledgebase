## Summary

This is a 28-day, four-times-daily m-Path protocol in a young, mostly student sample with common mental health problems. EMA completion was high. Engagement with the triggered intervention was low.

Completers answered a mean 98.21 EMAs (SD 12.69), or 87.69% of those scheduled. The range was 57% to 100%. By contrast, participants engaged with only 18.3% (mentalization) and 21.9% (mindfulness) of triggered interventions.

Retention was 89.2%. Three of nine withdrawals cited technical difficulties.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

BYOD. Inclusion required access to a personal smartphone. All participants downloaded m-Path and watched a training video.

EMAs came four times daily at fixed, evenly spaced times set around each person's reported sleep and wake times. Each prompt opened a two-hour window with a reminder at 60 minutes. Each EMA had ten single items rated 0 to 10.

The EMI fired when any expressed-emotion item deviated by more than 1 SD from the person's rolling average. It was delivered immediately after the EMA. Allocation was concealed inside the app's automated system, ordered by app download.

Staff sent email reminders when completion fell below 80%, at most once a week. The app also gave badges and data visualisations. The payment threshold was lowered from 80% to 60% before the trial, to capture more post-intervention feedback.

OS breakdown is not reported.

## Recruitment and Retention

| Stage | n |
|---|---|
| Assessed for eligibility | 280 |
| Met inclusion criteria | 99 (35%) |
| Consented and randomised | 84 (30%) |
| Disengaged, mentalization arm | 5 of 42 (11.9%) |
| Disengaged, mindfulness arm | 4 of 42 (9.5%) |
| Completed intervention and final assessment | 75 (89.2%) |

Recruitment ran March to December 2024. The target of ten per month was met overall. Recruitment slowed in summer.

Withdrawal reasons were technical difficulties (n=3), time constraints (n=1), not given (n=3), and one adverse reaction. The adverse reaction was increased body-image distress linked to the mindfulness arm's body focus.

Withdrawers were younger than completers by 3.78 years on average (mean 20.11 vs 23.91). No other baseline difference was found.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| EMAs completed per completer, mean (SD) | 98.21 (12.69) |
| Share of scheduled EMAs | 87.69% (SD 11.33%) |
| Range across participants | 57% to 100% |
| EMI triggered, mentalization / mindfulness | 79.4% / 78.6% |
| EMI engaged when prompted, mentalization / mindfulness | 18.3% / 21.9% |
| EMI prompts completed per participant, mean (SD) | 15.60 (22.74), range 0% to 100% |

Adherence is defined as engaging with at least 80% of EMAs over four weeks. The paper reports the mean completion figure rather than the share of participants above 80%.

The three technical-difficulty withdrawals are not described further. Qualitative feedback (32 of 75 completers) named EMA prompts as too frequent (n=10), confusing or irrelevant prompts (n=10), interventions not contextually relevant (n=12) and repetitive prompts (n=5).

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude that both EMIs are feasible and acceptable and warrant a definitive trial. Message intrusiveness and time burden were rated low (means about 2.4 to 3.0 on a 7-point scale).

They warn that retention and adherence may fall in routine use. The trial had financial incentives, monitoring with researcher reminders and human risk management, which a routine deployment would lack.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

High EMA completion did not carry over to the triggered intervention. A team planning a JITAI on m-Path should budget for EMI engagement near one in five prompts, not near the EMA rate.

A weekly email when completion drops below 80%, plus a payment threshold at 60%, coincided with 87.69% mean completion over 28 days. The paper does not isolate either element.

Expect some technical attrition even in a young BYOD sample. Three of 84 randomised left citing technical difficulties.

## Evidence Confidence

Verified for the CONSORT funnel, retention by arm, withdrawal reasons, EMA completion and EMI trigger and engagement rates. These were read from the Europe PMC full-text XML (PMC12483342) on 2026-10-31.

Unclear for the share meeting the 80% adherence definition. It is defined in Methods but not reported as a count.
