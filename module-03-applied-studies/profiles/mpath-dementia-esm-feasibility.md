## Summary

This module's cross-cutting patterns include a robust null — **baseline disease severity generally
does not predict attrition** — established across depression, ALS, bipolar disorder and psychosis.
This study extends it into the population where the assumption of infeasibility is strongest, and
where it has kept people out of ESM research entirely.

**Twelve people with mild-to-moderate dementia, given seven momentary prompts a day plus an
end-of-day survey for ten days, with a 20-minute total response window, completed 799 of 1004
prompts: a mean compliance of 80% (SD 13%, range 54%–91%). Nobody dropped out. Nobody was paid.**

The authors position 80% against the ~78% average for ESM studies generally, ~78% in mild cognitive
impairment, 71–80% in acquired brain injury and 54–99% in older adults — i.e. **at or above the
compliance of unimpaired samples**, achieved under a *more* restrictive response window than most.

Compliance correlated with **neither cognitive status (ACE-III, r = −0.13)** nor **functional ability
(DAD, r = 0.19)** nor **age (r = 0.41, p = 0.19)**. The only correlation approaching significance was
with the **FRS (r = 0.57, p = 0.05)** — and it points the "wrong" way for the infeasibility argument.
With n=12 these are all underpowered, and the authors say so.

What produced the result is not mysterious, and it is the operationally interesting part: **an
extraordinary amount of per-participant human tailoring**, described in enough detail to copy.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

**Fully remote**, exploiting the fact that participants already had recent full diagnostic workups at
the FRONTIER clinic — so no additional clinic visit was needed. Recruitment, consent, training and
follow-up were all by email and video call.

**Co-design came first.** The study design was presented to the **SDN-LEEAP lived-experience panel**,
whose four recommendations were all implemented: **Likert-scale buttons instead of sliders**; a
**video training tutorial** alongside written instructions; **extending collection from 7 to 10 days**
to capture more variability; and **recruiting a broad transdiagnostic sample** for inclusivity. The
panel also helped answer queries from the two ethics committees — a use of lived-experience input this
module has not previously recorded.

**Onboarding was staged and heavy:**

1. A screening phone/video call assessing smartphone use and proficiency **and mapping the
   participant's waking hours and daily schedule so the signalling schedule could be tailored**.
2. Written information, a consent summary, and a REDCap consent form to review at their own pace.
3. Written **and video** instructions for downloading m-Path, registering, entering the participant
   ID and granting permissions.
4. **A scheduled video call for a trial run** — a test notification, a practice survey with the
   researcher watching, and verification that **sound settings were adequate to hear the prompt**.
   Only then was a start date set.
5. **A follow-up video call after 2–4 days of live collection** to discuss burden and adjust.

**Process consent** was adopted rather than one-off consent, appropriate to the population.

**The per-participant adaptations are the study's real method, and each has a general lesson:**

- The first participant found Likert anchors ambiguous ("very" rather than "very happy"); the wording
  was changed **for her and all subsequent participants**.
- **Three participants needed longer to acclimatise** and reported dissatisfaction with their own
  early response rate; **the collection period was extended by 1–2 days** in consultation with them.
- **One participant could not remember to keep her phone nearby. At her partner's suggestion,
  notifications were synchronised to his phone so he could remind her.** Compliance improved markedly,
  without, by his report, undue burden on him.
- **One participant working part-time could not respond during work; the signalling schedule was
  modified on four working days** to reduce prompts during work hours.
- **The participant with the lowest compliance reported difficulty hearing the notifications** — which
  the authors identify as an opportunity they missed, since adjusting phone sound settings would
  likely have fixed it.

**Response window:** 5 minutes to first response, then a reminder, expiring 15 minutes later — **20
minutes in total**, which the authors correctly flag as more restrictive than most ESM protocols. The
end-of-day survey had a 30-minute reminder and the whole evening to complete.

**Completion times, measured:** momentary questionnaire including the voice recording **2 min 10 s
(SD 1 min 23 s)**; end-of-day items **36 s (SD 25 s)**. Both matched the pre-registered estimates.

## Recruitment and Retention

**23 contacted → 14 agreed (61%) → 12 analysed.** Reasons for declining, all reported: **concerns the
study would be too stressful or demanding (n=3)**; **rapid dementia progression with a move to
residential care (n=2)**; **too busy (n=1)**; one gave no reason; two never responded.

The authors compare their **61% agreement rate against 5–30% in general-population ESM studies** and
**36% and 14% in prior MCI studies**, and attribute it to participants already having an established
relationship with the research clinic and many having taken part in prior studies. That is a real
confound for generalisability and a real design lever: **recruiting from an existing research-engaged
clinic cohort multiplied the agreement rate several-fold.**

**Zero dropouts during data collection.** The end-point questionnaire is missing for 1 participant, so
the evaluation table is n=11.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

**Definition, stated precisely:** compliance = completed measurements ÷ possible measurements, where
possible = 7 momentary + 1 end-of-day prompts per day over the (occasionally extended) collection
period. **799 of 1004 = mean 80% (SD 13%, range 54%–91%).** No adjustment or filtering.

This is an unusually clean denominator by this module's standards — actual over scheduled, no wear-time
threshold, no valid-day rule, no partial-completion ambiguity. It is directly comparable to
[Clark 2025](metricwire-sgm-youth-ema-feasibility.md)'s 80.21% and stands in deliberate contrast to
[Dennard 2025](mpath-avatar2-esm-engagement.md)'s 39.1%, which is depressed by a platform
partial-save export limitation rather than by participant behaviour.

**No technical failure modes of the app are reported.** The problems that arose were human and
environmental: not hearing the notification, forgetting to keep the phone nearby, work schedules.
That is itself informative — a 10-day active-only protocol on a mature ESM platform did not encounter
the OS background-execution problems that dominate passive-sensing deployments, because **there is no
passive sensing here**. The contrast with
[Niemeijer 2023](carp-mpath-sense-performance-study.md) — the same platform family, with sensing
added, achieving ~0.50 coverage — is instructive: **the burden and fragility of a phenotyping study
sit almost entirely in the passive layer.**

## Feasibility Findings

**Subjective feasibility (18-item evaluation, 5-point Likert, n=11):**

- **Burden was low across all five burden items** (means 1.45–1.82; "disrupted my daily activities"
  1.82; "tiring to take part" 1.45; "stressful to use the phone app" 1.55).
- **Ease of participating was high**: "the app was easy to use" **4.55** (range 4–5); **"I sometimes
  needed help to complete the questions" scored 1.00 with zero variance** — not one participant ever
  needed help.
- **Carefulness was high**: "I answered the questions carefully" 4.64; "gave myself enough time" 4.64.
- **Motivation** 4.27; "taking part was important" 4.27; training helpfulness 4.18; feeling supported
  4.27.
- **Three items were only moderate**: enjoying tracking their thoughts (3.27), **remembering to keep
  the phone with them (3.82)**, and answering without self-reflection (2.09 reversed). The
  phone-proximity item corroborates the one participant who needed partner-synchronised reminders.

One participant found participation *"quite boring"*; several volunteered appreciation for the chance
to reflect. Two gave substantive methodological feedback on item ambiguity — evidence of engagement
with the research questions rather than mechanical responding.

**Utility:** variance decomposition showed sufficient within-person variability to justify an
intensive design, with **nine items showing ICC < 0.50** (more within- than between-person variance).
Daily meaning-in-life and life-satisfaction items had higher ICCs, as expected at lower measurement
frequency, but still varied meaningfully within person.

**The authors' own stated framework for replication:** engage a stakeholder panel to co-design;
adopt a flexible, individually tailored, supportive approach; use user-friendly technology; monitor
progress closely; provide step-by-step instructions in accessible formats; and involve informal
carers, who "played a positive role in encouraging their spouses or parents" — while noting that
**partner support was helpful but not essential**, since several participants completed the study
independently.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

1. **Mild-to-moderate dementia is not a contraindication for high-intensity ESM.** 7 prompts/day,
   10 days, 20-minute windows, 80% compliance, zero dropouts. Any protocol excluding this population
   on feasibility grounds should now justify that exclusion against this result.
2. **Compliance was uncorrelated with cognitive and functional status.** Underpowered at n=12, but the
   direction of the only near-significant correlation does not support a severity–compliance link.
   This extends the module's severity-does-not-predict-attrition null into a fifth condition.
3. **The 80% is bought with staff time, not money.** A screening call mapping the daily schedule, a
   video training tutorial, a supervised trial-run video call, a day-2-to-4 check-in call, and
   per-participant schedule modifications. **No compensation was offered at all.** Set against
   [Mercier 2020](beiwe-spinal-cord-injury-incentives.md) (incentives raise enrolment persistence but
   not completion rate) and [Shen 2026](sensorkit-techsans-older-adults.md) (an uncompensated passive
   study losing a participant to "lack of perceived benefit"), the pattern that best fits the module
   is: **structured human support raises completion; payment raises persistence; they are different
   levers for different failure modes.**
4. **Budget for schedule tailoring as a standard feature, not an exception.** Two of twelve
   participants needed the signalling schedule changed, and a third needed the collection window
   extended. A platform that cannot do this per-participant, mid-study, cannot run this protocol.
5. **Check the participant can hear the notification.** The lowest-compliance participant's problem
   was audibility, and the authors name it as an avoidable miss. Trivial, and it belongs on every
   onboarding checklist — compare LINC's device-configuration checklist in
   [Calvert 2026](mindlamp-linc-passive-data-quality.md).
6. **Carer-synchronised notifications are a viable adherence mechanism** in cognitively impaired
   populations, and one this module has not otherwise recorded.
7. **Recruiting from an existing research-engaged clinic cohort produced a 61% agreement rate against
   14–36% in comparable populations.** That is a bigger lever than anything in the protocol itself —
   and it is also the study's main threat to generalisability.
8. **A voice-recording item removes typing proficiency as a barrier** without measurable cost: the
   momentary survey including it still took 2 min 10 s.

## Evidence Confidence

**Verified** — the recruitment funnel (23/14/12) with all stated reasons for declining; zero dropouts;
the 799/1004 = 80% compliance with SD and range; all four compliance–covariate correlations with CIs
and p-values; the full 18-item evaluation table; the completion times; the protocol (7+1 prompts, 90-min
blocks, 15-min minimum gap, 5-min + 15-min response window); the four co-design recommendations and
their implementation; all five named per-participant adaptations; the ICC/variance result; the absence
of compensation; the diagnostic and cognitive characteristics of the sample. Read from the published
PDF (Nature/npj Dementia), 2026-09-02.

**Underpowered.** n=12. Every correlation reported here has wide confidence intervals and the authors
say explicitly that they "should be interpreted with caution given the small sample size."

**Selection is the main threat.** All participants were recruited from a single specialist research
clinic where they had existing relationships and many had prior study experience; the sample skews to
**young-onset dementia** (mean age 62), independent smartphone users, mild-to-moderate stage, and
83% living with a partner. **This is not evidence that ESM is feasible in dementia generally** — it is
evidence that it is feasible in a well-supported, research-engaged, digitally competent, young-onset
subgroup, with intensive individual tailoring. Exclusion criteria removed severe sensory impairment
and inability to use a smartphone independently.

**COI — direct and disclosed.** Egon Dejonckheere is **co-founder and shareholder of m-Path Software**,
the platform used. The first, third, fourth, fifth and sixth authors declare none. What tempers it:
the paper's claims are about the *population*, not the platform, and the platform's contribution is
mostly ordinary ESM delivery; the flexible scheduling and voice-recording features are named as
enabling but are not the object of a comparison. The claim most exposed to the COI is "leveraging
user-friendly technology" as one of the four replication ingredients.
