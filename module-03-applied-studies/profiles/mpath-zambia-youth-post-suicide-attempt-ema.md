## Summary

This is the first deployment in the module from sub-Saharan Africa. It is also the first to run EMA in the weeks after an emergency-treated suicide attempt in a low-resource health system.

Compliance was moderate. Of 560 scheduled prompts, 308 were answered (55%). The mean participant answered 14.4 prompts (SD 9.5) and had at least one answered prompt on 8.8 of 14 days. Retention was high by the paper's own definition, with 20 of 21 enrolled answering at least once.

The safety load was the striking operational figure. The emergency protocol fired 42 times among 18 participants in 14 days. Every activation got a call. Eleven calls went unanswered and eight were false activations from participants answering about the past rather than the present.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

BYOD. A research assistant met each participant in hospital after recovery and before discharge, obtained consent, ran a baseline survey and installed m-Path on the participant's phone. Inclusion required a web-enabled smartphone.

Prompts came twice a day for 14 consecutive days. The morning prompt went out at 06:00 with a reminder at 10:00 and stayed open until 12:00. The second prompt went out at 16:00 with a reminder at 20:00 and stayed open until 23:00. Each survey took about three minutes. m-Path required every item before submission, so completed prompts had no item-level missing data.

Participants received mobile data support. Compensation was 14 Kwacha (0.54 EUR) per answered prompt, paid as phone-data money. After the study, staff showed participants how to remove the app.

High-risk answers to Paykel scale items 4 or 5 triggered an in-app safety message and a call from trained clinical staff. Hospital-based "Champions" (doctors, nurses, clinical officers, registry staff) flagged eligible patients to the project manager.

OS breakdown is not reported. Recruitment dates are not reported.

Smartphone access excluded three of 28 eligible patients. A fourth had a phone that could not run the app. Together that is four of seven non-enrolments. The paper does not report how many patients were screened before the eligibility count.

Retention is defined as answering at least one prompt. A stricter retention definition is not given.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Scheduled prompts | 560 |
| Answered prompts | 308 (55%) |
| Prompts per participant, mean (SD) | 14.4 (9.5), median 14, range 0 to 28 |
| Days with at least one answered prompt, mean (SD) | 8.8 (5), median 9, range 0 to 14 |

A prompt counted as completed if at least one item was answered. Because m-Path forced full completion, that equals full surveys here.

The denominator does not reconcile with enrolment. 560 prompts equals 20 participants times 28 prompts. 21 enrolled participants would give 588. The paper does not explain the difference.

Named barriers were intermittent internet connectivity and phone charging. One participant attributed missed prompts to a network breakdown. One participant used a sister's phone and relied on her to wake them for the morning prompt. Two participants were unhappy with prompt timing.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude that smartphone EMA is feasible and acceptable in this population and setting. They call compliance moderate but comparable to high-income settings. They describe connectivity, device access and charging as modifiable implementation problems rather than limits of the method.

They name the emergency-response protocol as resource-intensive and a scalability problem in routine care. They recommend clearer instructions on reporting the present moment, research-assistant training, and adaptive EMA based on computerized adaptive testing to cut burden.

Participants described the prompts as supportive. Several said follow-up calls after activations encouraged continued participation.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

Budget clinical staff for the safety protocol as well as the app. The protocol fired 42 times among 18 of 21 participants in two weeks, and each activation needed a call.

Train participants on momentary framing before the first prompt. Eight of 42 activations were false because participants answered about the past.

Expect BYOD to exclude part of the clinical population in this setting. Four of 28 eligible patients could not take part because of phone ownership or compatibility.

Paying per prompt in mobile data money pairs the incentive with the resource the protocol consumes. The paper does not test whether this changed compliance.
