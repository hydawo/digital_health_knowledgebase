## Summary

This is the first EMA feasibility study in tuberous sclerosis complex, a rare genetic condition with epilepsy and neuropsychiatric features. It is in the module because it pairs a clinical group with matched controls on the same m-Path protocol and reports completion, retention and reasons for missed prompts.

Completion was high in both groups. Participants with TSC completed 896 of 1008 prompts (88.89%, range 60.71 to 100%). Participants without TSC completed 906 of 1008 (89.88%, range 76.79 to 100%). The difference was not significant (W = 148.5, p = .679). Nobody dropped out.

Within the TSC group, completion fell as symptom load rose. It was negatively associated with total current TSC symptoms (rho = -0.48, p = .043) and lifetime symptoms (rho = -0.54, p = .019). It was not associated with IQ, genotype, age or baseline mental health scores.

The protocol was co-produced. Two young adults with TSC sat on an advisory group and chose four prompts a day over more prompts on fewer days.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

Bring your own device. All participants had to own a smartphone already. The paper does not report the iOS and Android split.

m-Path sent four notifications a day. Each was semi-randomised within a two-hour block, at least one hour apart, across a maximum 14-hour day. A reminder followed 30 minutes after each prompt. The questionnaire expired 60 minutes after the first notification. Each questionnaire held up to 29 items depending on branching and time of day.

Each participant was scheduled 56 prompts. The study sent 2016 prompts in total.

The whole procedure was remote, by telephone and online. Training used Microsoft Teams with a visualiser.

## Recruitment and Retention

The text gives no counts for approaches, screening or refusals. Those are in a participant flowchart figure whose content is not in the preprint text.

Retention was 100%. No participant in either group withdrew. All participants completed more than the 33% inclusion threshold.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

Completion was defined as completed prompts divided by prompts sent. Overall completion was 1802 of 2016 (89.38%).

Each missed prompt was followed by the in-app question "Why did you miss the last prompt?" Sleeping was the most common answer in both groups. The second most common was socialising in the TSC group and studying in the comparison group.

Careless responding was flagged in 5.1% of valid responses (6.1% TSC, 4.1% non-TSC). Completion rate did not predict careless responding.

No technical failure modes are reported. App crashes, notification delivery problems, battery and phone compatibility are not mentioned.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude that smartphone EMA is feasible and acceptable for young adults with TSC. They base this on 100% retention, 89% completion, acceptable discriminant validity and low careless responding.

Usability was high on the PSSUQ. The TSC group's median total was 1.37 and the comparison group's mean was 1.38, with no significant difference.

In interviews, participants with TSC named fatigue, seizures and post-seizure confusion or memory loss as barriers. Other barriers were privacy in social settings, repetitive questions, fixed response options and the one-hour response window.

The authors recommend more activity categories, reordered questions to reduce monotony, and more flexible response formats.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

A rare-disease group with epilepsy reached the same completion as matched controls under a four-a-day, 60-minute-window m-Path protocol. The co-produced, lower-frequency schedule is a plausible contributor that the study did not test.

Symptom burden rather than diagnosis tracked completion. A future study in a neurological population could stratify on symptom load rather than diagnostic category.

The eligibility rules excluded roughly the third of people with TSC who have intellectual disability, and anyone without a smartphone. The authors flag both exclusions.

Two weeks is short. The authors say it cannot capture fluctuations such as seizure clustering over longer periods.
