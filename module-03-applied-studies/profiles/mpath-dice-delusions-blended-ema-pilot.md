## Summary

DICE adapts the SAVVy hallucinations protocol to delusions. The app phases are dense. Participants did six days of EMA at ten surveys a day with 39 items each. They then received five coping reminders and an eight-item survey each day for ten days after each of sessions two and three.

Mean EMA completion was 59%. Mean EMI completion was 72%. Every analysed participant cleared the trial's 33% EMA floor, which was needed to generate the personalised functional analysis. All ten used the app between sessions.

The cohort is small and selected. Most were male, highly educated and confident with the internet. The authors flag that this limits generalisation.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

The paper states that it "used the mPath app (KU Leuven)." A research assistant gave a ten-minute app training after baseline assessment.

Phase one was functional assessment. The six-day EMA block followed session one. Regression on each person's EMA data produced a personalised template of variables associated with delusion intensity.

Phase two applied coping strategies. Three individual strategies were programmed into the app with the participant. Daily coping reminders and a short monitoring survey then ran for ten days after sessions two and three. Sessions were used to adjust the EMI.

Inclusion required familiarity with a smartphone or willingness to learn. Whether phones were the participant's own is not stated. OS breakdown is not reported. Recruitment dates are not reported.

## Recruitment and Retention

| Stage | n |
|---|---|
| Screened | 38 |
| Included | 13 |
| Dropped out before the intervention (time restrictions) | 1 |
| Dropped out after session two (symptom exacerbation) | 1 |
| Withdrawn for insufficient smartphone literacy | 1 |
| Analysed | 10 |

Retention was 10 of 13 (77%). The symptom-exacerbation dropout was classed as an unrelated serious adverse event. That participant's appointments had been delayed by an infection, and he found the gap between sessions burdensome.

One participant was withdrawn mid-intervention because insufficient smartphone literacy had not been identified at screening.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

| Measure | Value | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Mean EMA completion | 59% | above 33% per participant |
| Participants above 33% EMA | 10 of 10 | above 80% of participants |
| Used the app between sessions | 10 of 10 | above 80% of participants |
| Mean EMI completion | 72% | not stated |

The paper reports means only. Per-participant ranges, completion over time and reminder-level engagement figures are not reported. The Discussion says reminder engagement "differed significantly" between participants without giving numbers.

No technical failures are reported. Participants named too much text, too many EMA surveys and too much scrolling as drawbacks. Reasons for non-engagement in sessions included concentration problems, forgetting materials and questionnaire length.

Therapist-rated session engagement was 4.50 to 4.70 on a 1 to 5 scale. Results give protocol adherence as 90% to 97%. The Discussion gives it as over 85%.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude that DICE is feasible and acceptable in chronic SSD outpatients with distressing delusions. All three progression criteria were met. They attribute lower EMA completion to the voluntary nature of the offer rather than to the reminders.

They recommend studying what drives engagement and testing strategies to reduce barriers to digital tools in SSD. They note that higher education and technology use are known to raise engagement in psychosis.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

A 39-item survey ten times a day for six days yielded 59% completion in this group. A team planning a lighter or longer protocol has no direct comparison here, because only one density was tested.

Screen smartphone literacy in practice, not by self-report. One of three losses was a participant who passed screening but could not use the phone.

Set the completion floor by what the analysis needs. DICE needed 33% EMA to build each person's functional template, and every analysed participant reached it.

## Evidence Confidence

Verified for the funnel, retention, the three progression benchmarks and the two completion means. These were read from the Europe PMC full-text XML (PMC11816215) on 2026-10-31.

Unclear for anything beyond the means. The paper reports no distribution of completion and no reminder engagement figures.
