## Summary

This study used m-Path as an intervention platform rather than as a pure research instrument. It sits in the module because it gives a full recruitment funnel, a precise dropout definition and per-questionnaire response counts in an unpaid, clinically unwell outpatient group.

The funnel lost people at every stage. 88 patients were introduced to the study. 57 started the app. 38 produced complete datasets. The largest single loss was 15 participants (26% of starters) who stopped using the app for a week or more.

Among completers, the mood questionnaires held up. Each of the three daily questionnaires could be answered at most 28 times. Completers answered the morning one a mean of 20.26 times (SD 7.4), the daytime one 19.39 times (SD 7.5) and the evening one 20.08 times (SD 7.8).

The full cohort looked weaker. Across 55 participants with data, each questionnaire was answered a mean of 14 to 15 times. Between 27% and 33% answered a given questionnaire 7 times or fewer.

The intervention content was used much less than the mood checks. 44% of the full cohort never opened a relaxation module.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

Bring your own device. Eligibility required owning a smartphone that could install the app, and adequate knowledge of the phone. The paper says m-Path apps run on Android and iOS. It does not report the OS split.

Installation was supervised. A participant who consented booked an appointment after a therapy session to install the app. They received a handout with an installation guide and manual. Staff noted phone numbers for a follow-up call after one week.

Three mood questionnaires a day were pushed by notification. Each had a six-hour availability window (6:00 to 11:59, 12:00 to 17:59 and 18:00 to 23:59). Each could be answered once a day.

Safety monitoring used the m-Path dashboard. Baseline and post questionnaires included PHQ-9 item 9 on suicidal thoughts. A positive response triggered an automated warning message and the department's standard operating procedure. That procedure was triggered for 14 of 57 participants (25%) at baseline and 11 of 57 (19%) at the post time point.

Participant identifiers were random codes generated by m-Path. Participants could delete their own data.

## Recruitment and Retention

Staff presented the study to all 88 patients with therapy appointments during the recruitment period. 57 (65%) consented. The 31 who declined completed an anonymous paper questionnaire on their reasons. Those results are deferred to a separate paper.

Completers were defined as anyone who used any app content at least once a week for all four weeks. On that definition, 40 of 57 (70%) were completers. Dropouts were defined as no app use for a full week, or an explicit request to stop. 17 of 57 (30%) were dropouts. The analysed sample is 38, because 2 completers did not finish the final questionnaire.

Reasons for dropout are not reported here. The authors say they are analysed in a separate paper by Kaufmann et al.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

Mood-questionnaire completeness is reported as count bands per questionnaire out of a maximum of 28. Among completers, 47% answered the morning questionnaire 22 to 28 times. 50% answered the daytime one that often, and 58% the evening one. 37 of 38 completers answered all three questionnaire types at least once.

Module use was low. Among completers, 26% never tried a module. Among the full cohort, 44% never did. The most used module was psychoeducational text, with 30 uses across the full cohort.

The activity button was used a mean of 12 times per completer (SD 15.72, median 6).

No technical failure modes are reported. The authors attribute "problem-free use" to the installation guide, the manual and intuitive content.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude the app was feasible, acceptable and satisfactory against their pre-specified criteria. They note that all 38 completers used it independently.

Of completers, 89% rated the app rather or very easy to use. 79% would use such a service again, and 79% would recommend it. Video and audio quality was rated rather good or good by 92% and 89%.

Three questionnaires a day was the most praised feature. 44% of those giving feedback (24 of 54) named the thrice-daily schedule as a positive. 84% of completers said the frequency was appropriate.

The authors suggest push reminders for the relaxation modules and more personalisation to raise module use.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

An unpaid, transdiagnostic outpatient group kept answering short mood checks on m-Path for four weeks if they stayed in. 30% of starters did not stay in.

Report on the full cohort, not only completers. Here completers answered each questionnaire a mean of 19 to 20 times. The full cohort's means were 14 to 15.

A dropout rule of one week without use is strict and simple. It produced a 30% dropout rate here and is easy to replicate.

Expect EMA engagement to outrun intervention-content engagement. Push-notified mood checks were answered far more often than self-initiated modules.

## Evidence Confidence
