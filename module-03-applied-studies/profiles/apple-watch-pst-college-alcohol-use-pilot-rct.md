## Summary

This is a small pilot that used a consumer smartwatch as the self-guided half of a 12-week therapy programme. Weeks 1 to 6 were weekly counselor-led problem-solving therapy over Zoom, with the participant's watch data reviewed in session. Weeks 7 to 12 ran on the watch alone, through research-team prompts and Apple's built-in activity notifications.

Retention was total. All 26 enrolled students completed all study procedures. The intervention arm attended all 6 counselor sessions.

The watch figures are the useful ones. Participants wore the watch an average of 6.2 days a week (SD 0.8). In the watch-only weeks, 11 of 12 completed at least 70% of prompted tasks each week. Overall engagement with prompted tasks was 82%.

The study fell short of its recruitment target. It planned for 44 and enrolled 26. The authors say recruitment "was more challenging than anticipated."

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

Hybrid. Each intervention participant was issued an Apple Watch and told to pair it with their personal phone. The paper does not report phone OS. Apple Watch pairing requires an iPhone, so the arm was in practice iPhone-only. The paper does not discuss this as an eligibility constraint.

Research access ran through a second pairing. Participants paired their watches with the PI's "ABSTAIN LAB Apple Team account." The authors say this limited the team's access to fitness activity, heart rate and sleep metrics. The paper does not describe the export route, the app used or the data schema.

The watch data served two purposes. The team used it to track adherence. Counselors used it to give feedback against personal goals of 7000 to 10,000 steps a day and 7 to 9 hours of sleep. Heart rate alerts flagged elevated stress.

In weeks 7 to 12, participants received standardized watch notification prompts written by the research team. They also received Apple's built-in notifications, such as ring closures and achievement badges. The paper does not say how the custom prompts were delivered to the watch.

The control arm watched a 20-minute online video and joined one 30-minute Zoom group discussion. Control participants were not issued a watch during the study.

## Recruitment and Retention

Recruitment combined online advertising with in-person recruitment at two Chicago universities. An initial REDCap screener led to a tele-orientation session and a baseline assessment.

| Stage | n |
|---|---|
| Screened | 132 |
| Excluded (ineligible or declined, often citing busy schedules) | 106 |
| Enrolled and randomized | 26 |
| Intervention / control | 12 / 14 |
| Completed all study procedures | 26 |

The limitations section reports that 7 of 33 (21%) initially recruited participants opted not to proceed during tele-orientation. That funnel does not reconcile with the 132 to 26 count in Methods. The CONSORT diagram (Figure 1) was not legible in the XML text.

Retention was 100%. Feasibility was defined only as the proportion enrolled and retained at study end. Attrition reasons do not arise.

The compensation was the device. Participants in both arms received an Apple Watch on completing study procedures. A cash amount is not reported. The authors do not discuss the watch as a retention incentive.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

Self-guided adherence in weeks 7 to 12 was defined as responding to at least 70% of prompted tasks per week. Tasks covered applying problem-solving steps, checking stress through heart rate, and progress on sleep and step goals.

| Measure | Intervention (n=12) |
|---|---|
| Counselor sessions attended, weeks 1 to 6 | 100% |
| Met the 70% weekly prompted-task rule, weeks 7 to 12 | 91.7% (11/12) |
| Overall engagement with prompted tasks | 82% |
| Watch wear, days per week, mean (SD) | 6.2 (0.8) |

The paper does not define a wear day. It does not say which weeks the wear figure covers. It reports no completeness figure for heart rate, sleep or step data.

No technical failure modes are reported. There is nothing on sync problems, device loss, charging burden or data gaps. The paper does not say whether any were looked for.

Acceptability on the Client Counseling Satisfaction Scale was 4.38 (SD 1.05) in the intervention arm and 3.85 (SD 1.09) in the control arm. The Methods say the scale was given to the intervention arm only.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude the intervention "is feasible, acceptable, and hypothesis generating among college students with AUD." They base feasibility on 100% enrollment and retention.

They attribute high adherence to the watch, weekly check-ins from the research team and the structured sessions. They do not separate these.

Their recommendations are for a powered RCT with an attention-matched control and 6 to 12 months of follow-up. They also suggest moving the intervention to other wrist-worn devices, such as Fitbit and Samsung Galaxy Watch, to widen reach.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

The watch-only phase is the transferable part. Six weeks of self-guided prompted tasks held 11 of 12 participants above a 70% weekly threshold after counselor contact stopped. That is a small sample with a lenient threshold. It still suggests a watch can carry a prompt schedule after a human-led phase ends.

Issuing an Apple Watch restricts a study to iPhone users. In a BYOD-phone design that is a silent eligibility filter. This paper does not report how many screened students were excluded for lacking an iPhone.

Pairing participant watches to a lab Apple Team account is an unusual access route. A team copying it would need details the paper does not give, including the consent wording, the export mechanism and what the team could see.

Giving every completer a watch is a strong incentive. It may explain part of the 100% retention. A future trial should report it as a cost line and test retention without it.
