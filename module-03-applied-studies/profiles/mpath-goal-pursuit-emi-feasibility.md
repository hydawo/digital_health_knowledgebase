## Summary

This is the earlier, nonclinical companion to the same lead author's 2025 m-Path pilot RCT. Its value is the development path. Each iteration cut EMA burden after poor compliance in the one before.

In the final pilot, participants were sent 112 EMA surveys. They completed a mean 85 (SD 33), or 76%. Ten of 73 (14%) were excluded from analysis for low compliance. Sixty-five (89%) completed post-intervention measures.

The abstract also says compliance "was high (n=63, 86%)." That count appears to be the 73 minus the 10 excluded. The paper does not state the threshold used for exclusion.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

BYOD. Inclusion required a personal Android or iOS smartphone. The paper states that EMA and EMI were delivered "using the m-path platform."

The pilot ran four EMA surveys a day at semi-random times inside a personal 12-hour waking window. After the baseline week, participants completed self-guided online training in COM-B goal setting and mental contrasting with implementation intentions. A strategy prompt then came at the first beep each day. Further prompts fired when goal pursuit fell 1 SD below the person's rolling average.

If a participant reported low goal pursuit, the follow-up goal questions were skipped. Earlier iterations had found them aversive.

Staff sent reminder emails during the intervention. The authors say these appeared particularly useful for compliance. The app also gave badges and data visualisation. The OS split is not reported.

## Recruitment and Retention

The text reports 73 starting the EMI and 65 (89%) completing post-intervention measures. The full funnel from screening to app set-up is in Figure 2, which was not read as data. The Discussion says there was "a high level of attrition between baseline measures and those setting up the app." The numbers for that step are not given in the text.

Reasons for attrition are not reported. Recruitment dates are not reported. Iteration-one and iteration-two sample sizes are in Multimedia Appendix 3, which was not retrieved.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| EMA surveys sent per participant | 112 |
| EMA surveys completed, mean (SD) | 85 (33), 76% |
| Excluded for low compliance | 10 of 73 (14%) |
| Mood-triggered prompts per participant, mean (SD) | 18 (13) over 21 days |

The second iteration tried to personalise triggers from baseline predictors of goal pursuit. Poor compliance defeated it. The pilot fell back to a single trigger variable.

The authors note that branching away goal questions after low goal pursuit left far fewer responses for the secondary network model. That is non-random missingness created by the design.

No technical failures are reported.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude that the JITAI was feasible and acceptable in a nonclinical adult sample. Participants asked for fewer notifications and more varied EMA items to avoid fatigue. The authors recommend piloting in a clinical sample before a full trial.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

Iterating the protocol before the pilot worked here. Each round cut burden after poor compliance, and the final round reached 76% over 28 days.

Branching that skips items after a given answer reduces burden. It also creates missing data that depends on that answer.

Record the attrition between consent and app set-up in the text, not only in a figure. This paper names it as high but does not give the count in prose.

## Evidence Confidence

Reported. The pilot's EMA counts and the post-intervention completion figure were read from the Europe PMC full-text XML (PMC10993123) on 2026-10-31. The paper reports exclusion and compliance figures that do not fully reconcile (63 at 86%, 10 excluded at 14%, and "all 73" completing a mean 76%).

Unclear for the screening-to-set-up funnel. It exists only in Figure 2 and was not extracted.
