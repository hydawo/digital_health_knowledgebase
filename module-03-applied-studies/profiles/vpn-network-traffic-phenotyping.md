## Summary

A genuinely new passive modality for this module, and one that inverts almost every operational
constraint the rest of the module documents.

Every smartphone phenotyping platform profiled here — Beiwe, mindLAMP, AWARE, RADAR-base, CARP — fights
the same fight: an app that the operating system wants to suspend, kept alive by participant
interaction or server pushes. This study sidesteps the app layer entirely by **operating at the
network layer**. A standard WireGuard VPN profile routes all traffic through a study server, which
logs metadata without ever seeing packet contents. It is OS-agnostic, needs no research entitlement,
no app-store review, no sensor permissions and no rooting, and **imposed <1% battery drain over 24 h
in lab testing**.

The trade is exact and the paper says so: **the very invisibility that produces the feasibility
produces the failure mode.** Participants forgot the VPN existed — which is why they kept it running,
and also why they did not notice when a phone reboot silently switched it off. One participant lost
**304 hours (~12.5 days)** that way.

Headline operational numbers: **93% retention among participants contributing any valid traffic
(27/29)**, **66% completion from consent to exit interview (25/38)**, **mean hourly coverage 74.1%
(median 77.1%, range 21%–100%)**, **SUS 78.08** and NASA-TLX workload roughly 10–20/100.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

**BYOD, no app installed by the study team.** Participants installed the official WireGuard client
and loaded a personalised configuration generated by a study web portal; installation status was
verified before data collection began.

**Privacy model, which is unusually strong and materially shaped adherence:** each participant
generated a **random participant ID with no stored mapping to identity**, and could regenerate it at
any time. The server logged only PID, timestamp, destination hostname and byte/packet counts. Payment
was decoupled from data via a completion code encoding the compensation amount without linking to the
PID. Participants received a plain-language briefing (slides, video, live demonstration) before
consent.

**Participant-facing transparency dashboard**, showing cumulative active days, compensation earned,
the hostnames and apps observed, and a 10-minute-interval activity heatmap. It served three purposes
at once — technical safeguard, payment tracker, and behavioural nudge — and, per the interviews, also
functioned as an unintended intervention (see below).

**Adherence support:** reminder emails every 2–3 days directing participants to the portal to verify
connection status. This was necessary because **VPN status visibility varies by handset**: older
iPhones show a persistent VPN status-bar icon, newer notched iPhones and most Android devices do not.

**Compensation:** an IRB-approved tiered schedule based on VPN connection duration. Exact amounts are
in a supplementary appendix, not the main text.

## Recruitment and Retention

The funnel is reported completely, which is rarer in this module than it should be:

| Stage | n |
|---|---|
| Outreach reached | 100 |
| Informed consent | 38 |
| Completed onboarding survey | 32 |
| Provided valid network traffic | 29 |
| Active >5 days | 27 |
| Exit survey | 26 |
| Exit interview | 25 |

**Two retention definitions, both stated:**

- **"User retention" = proportion contributing ≥5 days of traffic, of all who contributed any
  traffic** = **27/29 = 93%** (Wilson 95% CI 78–98%).
- **Consent-to-exit-interview completion** = **25/38 = 66%** (Wilson 95% CI 50–79%).

**A 27-point spread, both defensible, both in the same paper.** The authors quote the 93% against
prior literature ("median retention of approximately 5.5 days or completion rates near 50%") and it
is the figure the abstract carries. This is another instance of the module's denominator problem, and
here the choice is between an already-filtered denominator (contributed any traffic) and the honest
top of the funnel.

Comparisons between those who consented but did not continue and those who completed showed **no
systematic differences in department, major or university role**.

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

**Definition, stated precisely:** data coverage = proportion of **60-minute intervals within each
participant's own observed monitoring window** (first to last active hour) containing at least one
byte of VPN traffic. A "valid day" = any calendar day with at least one active hour. Because
onboarding was staggered against a fixed end date, coverage is computed **relative to individual
monitoring opportunity**, not against a nominal 14 days.

**Across the 24 single-time-zone participants:**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean coverage | **74.1%** |
| Median coverage | 77.1% (IQR 63.6–90) |
| Bootstrap 95% CI | 66.3–81.4% |
| Range | **21% – 100%** |
| Hours of monitored traffic per participant | mean 311.6 (~13 d), SD 3.5, range **121–496** |
| Valid days per participant | mean 16.2, SD 3.8, range 10–23 |
| Median coverage gap | **7.5 h** (IQR 2.8–23.8) |
| Maximum single gap | **304 h (~12.5 days)**, participant P05 |

**Coverage did not vary systematically by time of day or day type** — morning 71.1%, afternoon 75.9%,
evening 77.6%, night 71.9%; weekday 74.2%, weekend 74.0%. This is a meaningful negative result: unlike
app-based passive sensing, which typically degrades overnight or during charging, network-layer
capture failed uniformly rather than at behaviourally correlated times. **Missingness here is closer
to missing-at-random than in any other passive stream in this module.**

**Named failure modes, from the interviews:**

1. **Device reboot / battery death silently disabling the VPN**, with no notification and, on most
   handsets, no visible indicator. The dominant cause. *"My phone died and I didn't realize the VPN
   turned off. It took almost a day before I noticed"* (P6). *"I didn't know that once my phone died
   I would have to manually turn it back on"* (P7).
2. **Deliberate privacy-motivated pauses.** **3 participants reported discomfort during banking or
   similar tasks and 7 reported occasionally disabling the VPN in those contexts.** These selective
   pauses tended to correspond to longer coverage gaps.
3. **Perceived battery drain and network slowdown**, prompting intentional disabling — *"I noticed my
   battery draining really fast"* (P7). **Objectively, lab measurement showed <1% battery over 24 h.**
   The interruptions were driven by *perception*, not by measured resource use. This is a clean,
   transferable finding: perceived burden and actual burden diverged, and the perceived figure was
   the one that moved behaviour.
4. **Self-report of adherence did not match the logs** in either direction — participants both over-
   and under-estimated how long the VPN had run.

**Acceptability:** NASA-TLX low across all six dimensions (mental demand mean 2.69, physical 1.54,
temporal 2.92, performance 4.08, effort 3.85, frustration 2.88), corresponding to roughly 10–20/100
against a cited COVID-19 exposure-notification app's 25–35/100. **SUS mean 78.08 (SD 14.96, range
52.5–100)**, above the conventional 68 usability benchmark.

**Reactivity, reported as a benefit rather than a confound:** **12 participants reported increased
awareness of their own smartphone use** after seeing the dashboard, with some describing deliberate
behaviour change — *"Once I saw it was tracking my activity, I actually took a break from Instagram
for a week"* (P13). **19 wanted to continue past 14 days**; 4 more were neutral-permissive. Several
did in fact keep the VPN running past the study end simply because they never turned it off.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude that VPN-based network-layer monitoring is feasible and acceptable for
multi-week naturalistic observation, that acceptability was **actively negotiated** (selective
disabling during sensitive activity rather than withdrawal), and that user control over pausing
probably *increased* rather than decreased overall participation.

Their own stated priorities for the next iteration are **automated VPN reactivation after reboot and
user-facing notifications when connectivity lapses** — i.e., the exact keepalive problem that Beiwe
and CARP solved with server-side heartbeats, arriving independently for a third time in this module.

**Limitations they name:** network traffic cannot separate foreground use from background processes;
fully offline behaviour generates no trace; the Apple App Privacy Report is iOS-only and exposes only
a rolling 7-day window against a 14-day study; and the sample is students at a single elite US
institution, unusually familiar with VPNs and privacy trade-offs.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

1. **A network-layer VPN is a viable passive channel where the research question is temporal rhythm
   rather than sensor-specific behaviour.** No app, no permissions, no entitlement, no store review,
   OS-agnostic, <1% battery. For teams blocked by Apple SensorKit review or Google Play policy, this
   is a real alternative.
2. **It fails on reboot, silently, and the failure is invisible on most handsets.** Any deployment
   must build automatic reconnection and an explicit status indicator before it can be considered
   reliable. Reminder emails every 2–3 days were the mitigation here and still left a 304-hour gap.
3. **Missingness is close to uniform across time of day and day type.** That is a genuine analytic
   advantage over app-based passive sensing, where gaps cluster around charging, sleep and low
   engagement — and it should be checked, not assumed, in any new modality.
4. **Perceived battery drain drove disabling despite measured drain of <1%.** Measure the objective
   figure *and* ask participants what they believe, because the belief is what changes behaviour.
5. **Privacy-motivated selective pausing is a data-generating process, not noise.** 7 of ~25
   participants disabled monitoring during banking. Gaps in a network-traffic stream are
   systematically non-random with respect to *content category* even while random with respect to
   time. Do not treat a coverage gap as behaviour-neutral.
6. **Publish the funnel, not the filtered retention rate.** 93% and 66% are both in this paper and
   describe the same study.
7. **A participant dashboard is an intervention.** 12 of 25 reported changed awareness and some
   changed behaviour. In an observational rhythm study this is a threat to validity; the same feature
   was used deliberately as an engagement lever in
   [Calvert 2026](mindlamp-linc-passive-data-quality.md) and here. It cannot be both free and
   neutral.

## Evidence Confidence

**Verified** — the full funnel (100/38/32/29/27/26/25); both retention definitions with their Wilson
CIs; the coverage definition and all coverage statistics including the time-of-day and weekday/weekend
stratification table; hours and valid days per participant; median and maximum gaps; the NASA-TLX and
SUS scores; the <1% lab battery figure; the counts of participants reporting banking discomfort (3),
selective disabling (7), increased awareness (12) and willingness to continue (19); the privacy model
and compensation decoupling. Read from the PMC full-text XML (PMC13118141), 2026-09-02.

**Reported** — the qualitative attributions of coverage gaps to reboots and battery death. These come
from participant interviews, are consistent with the telemetry, and are highly plausible; they are
not independently instrumented.

**Unclear / not reported in the main text** — the iOS/Android composition of the cohort, and any
**OS-stratified coverage**. The paper notes that VPN-status visibility differs by handset generation
and OS, which makes an OS breakdown especially relevant to the module's unresolved iOS/Android
question (Tier 15 Q111) — and it is not provided. Exact compensation amounts are in a supplementary
appendix only.

**Generalisability is narrow.** Students at one elite US university, self-selected, technically
literate, recruited through academic advisors. The authors say plainly that comfort with passive
monitoring and mental models of surveillance may differ substantially in other populations.

**COI** is minor and disclosed (two authors have a financial interest in Life Analytics Inc); neither
is first or last author and no commercial product is evaluated.
