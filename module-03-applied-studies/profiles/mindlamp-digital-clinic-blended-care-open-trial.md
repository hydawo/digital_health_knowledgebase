## Summary

This mindLAMP deployment is a clinical service, not a research study. The app was a tool inside a telehealth therapy programme. A team of 16 non-clinician digital navigators supported app use.

Retention was 83.3% (215/258) over 8 weeks. Nobody was paid. That is the number a clinical service can expect when the app is part of treatment rather than an add-on study.

App adherence was moderate. 73.5% (158/215) of completers used mindLAMP on at least half of their days in the clinic. The benchmark was 70%.

The weakest link was follow-up after care ended. Questionnaire completion fell from 83.3% at the end of treatment to 39.1% at 3 months. Outreach at 3 months was a single email.

Passive data completeness is not reported. The paper names the sensors collected but gives no coverage figures.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

BYOD. Inclusion required ownership of an Android or Apple phone (Textbox 1). OS split is not reported.

A digital navigator held a virtual introductory meeting with each eligible referral, set up the app, and ran weekly check-ins. Navigators were college students or recent graduates with no clinical experience (Methods, Participants). Each had 10 hours of in-person training over 4 sessions, then 2 supervised live appointments before working alone.

Navigator check-ins covered app setup and demonstration, review of data highlights and technology troubleshooting. Each check-in followed a templated checklist.

Clinicians were 10 people. Most were master's or doctoral counselling students. They had about 8 hours of training on the Unified Protocol manual plus weekly supervision.

In therapy sessions, clinicians screen-shared mindLAMP data with the patient, including symptom graphs and home-practice data (Intervention Description). Home practice was assigned through the app.

Phenotyping outputs shared with patients were steps, movement, screen time and a sleep estimate. mindLAMP also offered opt-in Apple SensorKit metrics. Whether any participant opted in is not reported.

## Recruitment and Retention

Recruitment was by primary care referral only (Recruitment and Enrollment Procedures). A psychiatrist on the team gave information sessions to PCPs twice a year. There was no direct outreach to patients. Referred patients got an emailed web screening form.

Recruitment rate was 72.1% (289/401) of those approached after eligibility was confirmed. 87.8% (289/329) of those who attended the introductory meeting enrolled. Both exceeded the 70% benchmark.

Retention was 83.3% (215/258). The definition is completion of the whole 8-week programme. The 31 people still in treatment were excluded from the denominator.

Reasons for non-completion are not reported. Figure 2's flowchart was not available as text.

Seven participants had significant life events during care. Five were bereavements, one was eviction and one was a fire. They were offered care but excluded from efficacy analyses.

Participants were not compensated. PCPs reported that endorsing the clinic directly seemed to raise patients' willingness to start (Lessons Learned).

## Data Completeness and Technical Issues

App adherence was defined as using mindLAMP for home practice (self-monitoring or skills practice) on at least half the days in the clinic. 73.5% (158/215) met it (Results). The per-participant distribution is not reported.

Questionnaire completion among completers was 100% (215/215) at baseline, 83.3% (179/215) at end of treatment and 39.1% (84/215) at 3 months. Among the 163 with clinical baseline scores, 3-month completion was 41.7% (68/163). The 3-month figure missed the 80% benchmark.

The app partly backfilled missed end-of-treatment questionnaires. For the 36 who skipped it, the team used in-app PHQ-9 and GAD-7 scores from within 10 days of the last session for 27 of them (Data Analytic Plan).

Digital access hurdles were asked of the 179 end-of-treatment respondents. 72.1% (129/179) reported no significant hurdle, just under the 75% benchmark. 11.2% (20/179) reported difficulty using mindLAMP. 10.6% (19/179) had difficulty finding a quiet place for sessions. 3.4% (6/179) had unstable Wi-Fi. 2.2% (4/179) had trouble remembering home practice.

The paper gives two figures for mindLAMP difficulty. Results says 11.2% (20/179). Lessons Learned says 3.4% (6/179), which matches the Wi-Fi count in Results.

Navigator fidelity was high. 98.9% (196/198) of a random 10% sample of check-in checklists showed perfect adherence.

Not reported. Passive sensor completeness for any stream. OS split. App crashes, sync failures or server problems. How many navigator contacts were spent on technical support.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude the Digital Clinic model "is feasible, acceptable, and potentially efficacious" and warrants an RCT. They judge feasibility good to excellent on most benchmarks.

Mean satisfaction was 4.81 (SD 0.52) for clinicians, 4.61 (SD 0.62) for navigators and 4.18 (SD 0.79) for the mindLAMP user experience, on 5-point scales. Midpoint WAI-SR was 50.15 (SD 7.86) and Digital Working Alliance Inventory 32.10 (SD 6.74).

Their stated lessons follow. Replace email-only 3-month follow-up with payment or a pre-booked appointment. Use part of the introductory meeting to solve practical barriers such as a private place for sessions. Partner with primary care, because PCP endorsement seemed to raise willingness to start.

They also report that some clinicians needed more training to integrate phenotyping data into care. The extent of data use in sessions was not formally tracked.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

A service or study that embeds mindLAMP in care with navigator support can expect about 83% completion of an 8-week course without payment. The figure comes from completers of an active clinical service, with people still in treatment removed.

Expect about three quarters of patients to use the app on half or more of their days. That is home-practice use, not passive coverage.

Do not rely on email for post-treatment follow-up. Response dropped to 39% at 3 months.

Budget for navigators. Sixteen volunteers each received 10 hours of training plus supervised sessions. Their time per patient is not reported.

If passive data are part of the outcome, plan to measure coverage yourself. This paper gives none.
