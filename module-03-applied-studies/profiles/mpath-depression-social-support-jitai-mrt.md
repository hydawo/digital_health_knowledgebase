## Summary

This is a six-times-daily, 21-day m-Path protocol in people with depressive symptoms who were waiting for therapy. It adds a JITAI that prompted participants to seek social support.

Mean compliance was 85.37% (SD 16.24%). Twenty of 25 participants (80%) cleared the 70% threshold set in advance. Careless responding on an attention check was 6 of 389 responses (1.5%).

Technical functioning was rated 6.36 on a 7-point scale. The only device-related losses were two participants who lost their smartphones mid-study.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

BYOD. Inclusion required owning a smartphone. Participants installed m-Path at an in-person meeting that also covered consent, a baseline survey and a qualitative interview.

Six EMA surveys a day were randomly prompted inside two-hour blocks from 8 AM to 8 PM. An evening survey came between 8 PM and 9 PM. A weekly survey came every seven days between 9 PM and 11 PM.

JITAIs were triggered from EMA answers under four microrandomized strategies. The trigger counts name three of them, a fixed cutoff, a statistical process control (SPC) rule and a self-reported support-need item. JITAIs were capped at two per day.

Ten days in, participants were told their personal participation rate and reminded of the compensation link. OS breakdown is not reported.

JITAIs fired 377 times. 221 came from the fixed cutoff, 110 from SPC and 46 from support need. A further 76 were suppressed by the two-per-day cap, affecting 15 participants on 55 intervention days.

Technical functioning was rated 6.36 (between-person SD 1.39). No app failure, sync problem or data loss is reported. Two smartphones were lost.

Evening compliance was much lower than daytime compliance. The paper reports the evening count but not an evening compliance percentage.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude that the JITAI is practically feasible in daily life. Participants adopted the suggested support-seeking in 29% of situations on average. Burden was rated low.

They note that in-person interviews may have raised engagement and lowered attrition. Interventions triggered by the support-need item were rated most appropriate.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

Six prompts a day for three weeks held 85% mean compliance in a clinical waitlist sample. That came with compliance-scaled pay and a day-10 feedback message on participation.

If a JITAI has a daily cap, expect the cap to bind. Here 76 triggers were suppressed, against 377 delivered.

Device loss was the only hardware attrition. Two participants lost their phones during the EMA phase and dropped out.
