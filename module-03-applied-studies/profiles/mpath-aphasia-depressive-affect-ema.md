## Summary

People with aphasia are usually excluded from depression research because standard questionnaires depend on language. This study adapted an m-Path EMA so that they could self-report.

Participants answered a mean 50 of 56 surveys (SD 5.20), a compliance rate of 89.6%. Compliance was not associated with aphasia severity, cognition or depression score. They took a mean 6 minutes 38 seconds to open a survey and 1 minute 27 seconds to complete it.

Recruitment was the hard step. Forty-three of 70 people contacted declined.

## Instrumentation and Deployment Model

BYOD. Inclusion required owning a smartphone. A researcher helped each participant download m-Path and create an account in a training session over Zoom. Care partners attended to help when needed. Participants did a practice survey before the protocol began.

Surveys came at 9 AM, 1 PM, 5 PM and 9 PM for 14 days, 56 in all. Each stayed open for one hour, with reminders at 30 and 50 minutes. Each survey had eight items. Six rated mood on a 5-point pictorial face scale. Two asked location and company.

Every item came with on-screen text, an illustration and a verbatim audio recording. Items and the response scale were chosen through focus groups with people with aphasia, care partners and speech-language pathologists, then tested in cognitive interviews.

All sessions ran remotely over Zoom. OS breakdown is not reported.

Compliance did not correlate with WAB-AQ aphasia severity (rho 0.16), CLQT cognition (rho 0.14) or CESD-R depression (rho -0.1).

Four participants reported timing problems with the 9 PM survey because they were usually asleep. Compliance at 9 PM was still high. Some found the 5-point face scale too limited to show what they felt.

No technical failures are reported.

## Feasibility Findings

The authors conclude that the EMA is user-friendly, accessible and well received, with high compliance. They recommend recruiting more people with severe aphasia, since only four took part. They suggest adding passive phone or wearable data as nonlinguistic indicators of mood.

## Relevance to Future Study Design

Multimodal items with audio, plus a researcher-led set-up with a care partner present, supported 89.6% compliance in a language-impaired group. Severity did not predict compliance.

Expect a large refusal step from a research registry. Forty-three of 70 contacted declined before any app use.

Check the last prompt of the day against participants' sleep times. Four of 27 found 9 PM too late.
