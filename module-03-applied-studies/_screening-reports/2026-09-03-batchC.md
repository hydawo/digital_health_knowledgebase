# Module 3 screening — Batch C (Onnela Lab early Beiwe papers, 2015–2019)

Screened 2026-09-03 from the complete pdftotext extractions in `txt/`. Rubric: `RUBRIC.md`.
None of the eight DOIs is in `module-03-applied-studies/literature-index.json`; all eight are
catalogued in `module-02-digital-phenotyping/literature-library.md` (L092, L097, L101, L103, L105,
L108, L112, L114 plus the earlier beiwe/ entry for Torous 2016).

## Headline finding: one schizophrenia deployment, four papers

Staples 2017, Barnett 2018 (NPP), Torous 2018 (npj Digit Med) and Barnett 2018 (JAMIA) all analyse
**the same deployment**: the BIDMC / Boston state outpatient mental-health-clinic Beiwe pilot in
schizophrenia, protocol published as Torous 2016 (JMIR Ment Health 3:e16), NIH DP2MH103909
(PI Onnela), IRB at Beth Israel Deaconess. Evidence that they are one cohort:

- Identical enrolment (17 subjects), duration (up to 3 months), setting ("state mental health
  clinic", Boston), sampling configuration (GPS 60 s on / 600 s off; accelerometer 60 s on / 60 s
  off at 10 Hz), survey schedule (three per week), relapse count (5), and funding line.
- Staples 2017 Methods: "followed the protocol outlined in an associated protocol paper.29"
  (ref 29 = Torous 2016). Torous 2018 Results: "The study protocol is explained in detail in
  ref. 10" (= Torous 2016) and cites Staples 2017 as "this patient cohort elsewhere.11".
  Barnett 2018 JAMIA: "we evaluated 17 patients with schizophrenia for up to 3 months ... .2"
  (= Torous 2016) and "Using our schizophrenia cohort as an example".
- The head-counts differ only by the analytic cut: 17 enrolled (Staples, Barnett NPP, JAMIA);
  16 with any usable data (Torous 2018, after excluding the one subject who never connected to
  Wi-Fi); 15 analysed (Barnett NPP, after also dropping one with < 1 week of follow-up).

**Module 3 has no profile for this cohort** (checked: `dp-schizophrenia-tolerability.md` is Raugh
2021, mEMA + Empatica, University of Georgia; `sleepsight-schizophrenia-rest-activity.md` is Meyer
2018, Purple Robot + Fitbit, London). So the cohort is new. Recommendation: **one profile**, anchored
on Torous 2018 (npj Digit Med), with Barnett 2018 (NPP) and Staples 2017 as co-sources. Details and
per-paper verdicts below. Barnett 2018 (JAMIA) is a statistics review that only uses the cohort for
simulation and is rejected.

## 1. Torous 2016 — New tools for new research in psychiatry (Beiwe platform paper)

- **File:** `2016-torous-jmirmentalhealth-new-tools-for-new-research-in-psychiatry.txt`
- **First author (byline):** John Torous. Co-authors Kiang, Lorme, Onnela.
- **Year / journal:** 2016, *JMIR Mental Health* 3(2):e16
- **DOI:** 10.2196/mental.5165. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform per Methods:** Beiwe (Android 1.0 on a Samsung Galaxy S4, Android 5.0; iOS "under
  development and will be completed early 2016"). Quote: "We report on the development and early
  testing of Beiwe, a research platform featuring a study portal, smartphone app, database, and data
  modeling and analysis tools". Matches filename.
- **Verdict: REJECT — architecture.** (Alternative code: protocol.) It is the platform-introduction
  paper plus a *proposed* pilot ("we are in the process of starting a clinical study ... we will
  enroll 20 patients"). No cohort, no results. The only deployment content is internal testing by
  "numerous healthy volunteers" (no N) reporting no noticeable battery drain, and the claim that a
  subject never connecting to Wi-Fi "has never happened in either test use or in our other ongoing
  studies" — a claim later contradicted by Barnett 2018 NPP (one of 17 never connected). Worth
  quoting in the cohort profile as the protocol's expectation vs. what happened.
- **Existing Module 3 profile covering same cohort:** none (no cohort). Its planned design
  (N=20, 30-day blocks x3, daily WSS + biweekly PHQ-8/PSQI/GAD-7, daily audio, flat-rate phone
  reimbursement, Bluetooth/Wi-Fi deliberately not collected) is the protocol for the cohort profiled
  under papers 3, 5 and 6. Note the executed study differed: 17 enrolled not 20; surveys three
  times a week (Mon/Wed/Fri) not daily; no audio results reported in any of the three cohort papers.

## 2. Torous 2015 — Personal-smartphone PHQ-9 app in MDD (Mindful Moods)

- **File:** `2015-torous-jmirmentalhealth-utilizing-personal-smartphone-custom-app-assess.txt`
- **First author (byline):** John Torous. Co-authors Staples, Shanahan, Lin (Pocket Gems), Peck,
  Keshavan, Onnela.
- **Year / journal:** 2015, *JMIR Mental Health* 2(1):e8
- **DOI:** 10.2196/mental.3889. **PMID** 26543914 printed; **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform per Methods:** NOT Beiwe. Quote: "Subjects were directed to download the app, Mindful
  Moods, onto their personal smartphone from either the Apple iTunes or Android Google Play store."
  A custom, survey-only app (log-in screen + one survey screen), no passive sensing. The filename
  says "custom app", which is accurate; the folder (`onnela-lab/`) is what could mislead.
- **Verdict: REJECT — instrument is not a Module 1/2 profiled platform.** No rubric code fits
  exactly (closest in spirit: no-cohort is wrong; it *has* a cohort). Per README this is formally a
  "Module 2 expansion candidate", but Mindful Moods was a one-off pilot app with no later use, so
  it is not worth a Module 2 profile. Do not absorb into Module 3.
- **For the record (out of scope, not for a profile):** 14 offered, 13 enrolled (93%); 29–30 days;
  BYOD iOS + Android; adherence 903/1161 sessions = 77.78% (morning 75.5%, afternoon 84.2%,
  evening 75.5%); $50 contingent on >= 70% completion; no subject used the support contact.
  Adherence dipped and stabilised after ~2 weeks. Also the origin of the observation, reused by
  Berry 2019, that app-reported scores exceed clinic scores (+3.02 PHQ-9 points; 9/13 reported
  suicidal ideation on the app vs 0/13 on paper).
- **Existing Module 3 profile covering same cohort:** none.

## 3. Staples 2017 — Passive vs active sleep estimates, schizophrenia cohort

- **File:** `2017-staples-npjschizophrenia-comparison-passive-active-estimates-sleep-cohort.txt`
- **First author (byline):** Patrick Staples (Staples and Torous contributed equally).
- **Year / journal:** 2017, *npj Schizophrenia* 3:37
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41537-017-0038-0. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform per Methods:** Beiwe, BYOD. Quote: "17 subjects with a diagnosis of schizophrenia
  currently in treatment downloaded Beiwe, a platform for digital phenotyping, on their personal
  Apple or Android smartphones." Matches filename.
- **Verdict: BORDERLINE-include — as a co-source for the single schizophrenia-cohort profile,
  not a standalone profile.** Its research question is sleep validity (PSQI vs EMA vs
  accelerometer), which alone would be Module 1-style validation territory, but it carries three
  deployment figures the other cohort papers do not: the accelerometer data-availability
  distribution, the EMA completion rate, and the compensation model. Same cohort as papers 5
  and 6 (see headline).
- **Existing Module 3 profile covering same cohort:** none yet.

### Extraction (Staples 2017)

- **Design:** "three-month-long study was strictly observational" (Methods, p.5).
- **Funding / COI:** NIH/NIMH 1DP2MH103909-01; Harvard McLennan Dean's Challenge; Myrtlewood
  Foundation (MK, LS, JT); NLM 4T15LM007092-25 (JT). "no competing financial interests" (p.5).
  Onnela (Beiwe originator) senior author.
- **Population / setting / country:** "17 subjects with a clinical diagnosis of schizophrenia from
  a state outpatient mental health clinic" (Methods, p.5); Boston, USA. Mean age 26.4; 15/17
  (88.2%) male; race: 6 Caucasian, 4 Hispanic, 3 African American, 2 East African, 1 Caribbean,
  1 East Asian (Results, p.2).
- **Eligibility:** "owned a smartphone capable of running the Beiwe app (any Android or iOS phone
  running 5.0 or 9.0 or higher, respectively), their clinician at the clinic approved of their
  participation in the study, and they were able and willing to provide written informed consent"
  (Methods, p.5).
- **Enrolled -> analysed:** 17 enrolled; "13 subjects who completed at least one in-person PSQI"
  used for the PSQI comparison (85% = 11/13 classified correctly, Abstract). Why 4 had no PSQI is
  **not reported**. Figure IDs run 1–19 because "all platform registrations, including test
  phones" were included (p.2).
- **Duration:** 3 months; "At the time of analysis, over 90% of subjects used the Beiwe platform
  for over 6 weeks" (Results, p.2). Exact retention at 3 months: **not reported**.
- **Deployment model:** BYOD, iOS and Android; installed "during the initial visit" (Methods).
  No custom integration.
- **Recruitment:** clinic-based; funnel (approached/refused) **not reported**.
- **Retention definition / rate:** only the "> 90% used ... for over 6 weeks" statement; no
  definition given. Attrition reasons **not reported**.
- **Active-data completeness:** "approximately half of all phone EMAs were co[mpleted]" (Results,
  p.2; the extracted text is truncated at "co:") and "only approximately 50% of EMAs sent to the
  subjects were completed" (Discussion, p.4). Three EMAs per week, seven symptom questions each;
  four sleep items weekly.
- **Passive-data completeness:** "Across all subjects, mean proportion of accelerometer data is
  46%, with a range of 2.1–89.1%" (Fig. 1 caption, p.2). Denominator = scheduled on-cycles over 30
  days. "accuracy of accelerometer-based estimates ... inversely related to accelerometer
  missingness" (p.3). No OS breakdown here (see Torous 2018 for that).
- **Technical failure modes documented:** accelerometer missingness "may have been due to subjects
  turning off their phones, losing their phones, or other unexpected uses" (Discussion, p.5); power
  state "is not gathered continuously, but rather only power state toggling ... some values of this
  toggling are not recorded" (Methods, p.5); GPS judged unsuitable for sleep because some subjects
  rarely leave home.
- **Compensation / support model:** "$25 for attending each study clinical visit, and ... $25 per
  month for use of their personal smartphone, although this compensation was not tied to app use.
  Subjects had the ability to call the study investigator for assistance if needed, although no
  additional coaching, therapy services, check in calls, or appointments were associated with this
  study" (Methods, p.5). Subjects could not view their data until study end.
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions:** "sleep monitoring via personal smartphones is feasible for
  subjects with schizophrenia in a scalable and affordable manner" (Abstract); but "may not be as
  accurate, limiting feasibility" because of missing accelerometer data (Discussion, p.5).
- **Not reported that a designer would want:** number approached; 3-month retention; per-OS
  counts; GPS/call-log completeness; reasons for the 4 subjects with no PSQI; battery complaints.

## 5. Barnett 2018 — Relapse prediction in schizophrenia (Neuropsychopharmacology)

- **File:** `2018-barnett-neuropsychopharmacologyo-relapse-prediction-schizophrenia-through-digital-phenotyping.txt`
- **First author (byline):** Ian Barnett (Barnett and Torous contributed equally).
- **Year / journal:** 2018, *Neuropsychopharmacology* 43:1660–1666; published online 22 Feb 2018.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41386-018-0030-z. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform per Methods:** Beiwe, BYOD. Quote: "Seventeen patients with schizophrenia in active
  treatment at a state mental health clinic in Boston used the Beiwe app on their personal
  smartphone for up to 3 months per subject." Matches filename.
- **Verdict: INCLUDE — as co-source for the single cohort profile (same deployment as papers 3
  and 6).** It is the only paper of the four that gives the attrition funnel with reasons, and it
  documents two failure modes (never-on-Wi-Fi, app uninstall before relapse) that no other paper
  in the set reports. Under the rubric this is "duplicate cohort but adds new operational figures,
  so include and note the overlap".
- **Existing Module 3 profile covering same cohort:** none yet.

### Extraction (Barnett 2018 NPP)

- **Design:** observational pilot; anomaly-detection analysis (Hotelling's T2 on detrended
  features).
- **Funding / COI:** NIH/NIMH 1DP2MH103909 (PI Onnela), Harvard McLennan Dean's Challenge, Natalia
  Mental Health Foundation, Dupont-Warren Fellowship, Brain and Behavior Research Foundation Young
  Investigator (JT). "The authors declare no competing interests." Onnela senior author.
- **Population / setting / country:** schizophrenia, active treatment, state mental health clinic,
  Boston, USA. Demographics not repeated here (see Staples 2017).
- **Enrolled -> analysed (Methods, p.1662):** 17 enrolled -> "1 of the 17 subjects never connected
  his phone to WiFi, his data are unfortunately not available for analysis" -> 16 -> "1 had less
  than a week of follow-up" -> **15 analysed**, "followed for 1075 person-days with a median
  follow-up time of 79 days".
- **Duration:** up to 3 months.
- **Deployment model:** BYOD, "runs on both Android and Apple smartphones, we did not exclude any
  subjects based on the type of phone they owned" (Discussion, p.1663). No custom integration.
- **Support / incentive model (verbatim, Methods p.1662):** "The subjects were not paid for app use,
  not given additional support for app use, and not provided with check in calls or study staff
  reminders to use the app. We configured Beiwe to use WiFi rather than cellular network for data
  uploads in order to eliminate data charges to the subjects."
- **Sampling:** "GPS data for 1 min every 10 min in order to minimize battery drain"; surveys
  "twice per week" (note: Staples 2017 and Torous 2018 say three times per week, Mon/Wed/Fri — a
  within-cohort inconsistency to record, not resolve).
- **Recruitment:** clinic; funnel **not reported**.
- **Retention / attrition:** no formal retention rate. Relapse-linked attrition: of 5 relapses, "one
  uninstalled the smartphone application 3 weeks prior to hospitalization, and the other
  uninstalled the application 1 week prior to hospitalization" (Results, p.1663). Follow-up for the
  three analysable relapsers: 48 (37 pre-hospitalisation), 53 (48), 79 (41) days.
- **Data completeness:** "Despite adhering to the study and having reliable smartphone data
  collection for the first month, no data were collected for 2 days prior to the significant
  behavioral anomalies" (p.1663). Minutes of GPS data missing is itself a modelled feature
  (Table 2, feature 13). Overall completeness percentages **not reported** here (see Torous 2018).
- **OS breakdown:** counts **not reported**; sociability features only from Android call/text logs.
- **Technical failure modes:** (1) Wi-Fi-only upload -> total data loss for a participant who
  never joined Wi-Fi; (2) participant-initiated uninstall in the run-up to relapse (2 of 5
  relapsers); (3) "heterogeneity of smartphone devices and operating systems ... sensors and
  operating systems are not identical in all phones" (Discussion, p.1665).
- **Acceptability:** "No subjects indicated that passive data made them feel paranoid or afraid"
  (p.1665).
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions:** "Our results support the feasibility of digital phenotyping
  in psychotic disorders"; use of personal phones "is the only way to scale these types of studies
  to larger cohorts and longer follow-up times" (p.1665). Sensitivity/specificity "cannot be
  accurately estimated from our data".
- **Not reported:** approached/refused; per-OS N; overall passive completeness; battery or
  participant complaints; why the < 1-week subject stopped.

## 6. Torous 2018 — Clinical relevance of digital-phenotyping data quality (npj Digit Med)

- **File:** `2018-torous-npjdigitalmedicine-characterizing-clinical-relevance-digital-phenotyping-data.txt`
- **First author (byline):** John Torous (Torous and Staples contributed equally).
- **Year / journal:** 2018, *npj Digital Medicine* 1:15
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41746-018-0022-8. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform per Methods:** Beiwe, BYOD. Quote: "Sixteen outpatients in a state mental health
  clinic in active treatment with confirmed diagnosis of schizophrenia used the Beiwe smartphone
  app,10 ... Patients installed the Beiwe smartphone app onto their personal Android or iOS
  smartphones." Matches filename.
- **Verdict: INCLUDE — anchor paper for the new schizophrenia-cohort profile.** Data quality is
  its explicit subject: it is the only paper in the cohort set that quantifies passive coverage
  against the configured schedule, gives an OS breakdown of coverage and survey latency, and
  states survey adherence with a definition. It also carries the "stopped surveys, passive kept
  flowing" pre-relapse pattern that the Beukenhorst ALS profile reports in the opposite direction.
- **Existing Module 3 profile covering same cohort:** none.

### Extraction (Torous 2018)

- **Design:** pilot, observational; linear mixed models of lagged data-quality metrics vs weekly
  survey scores.
- **Funding / COI:** NIH/NIMH 1DP2MH103909-01 (PI Onnela), Harvard McLennan Dean's Challenge, Natalia
  Mental Health Foundation, NLM 4T15LM007092-25, Dupont Warren Fellowship. "The authors declare no
  competing interests." Onnela senior author; the paper is the platform originators reporting their
  own platform's coverage.
- **Population / setting / country:** 16 outpatients with schizophrenia, state mental health clinic,
  Boston, USA; "primarily male"; no healthy controls (Discussion, p.7).
- **Enrolled -> analysed:** 16 with data (the 17th, never on Wi-Fi, is excluded upstream — Barnett
  NPP). "Of the 14 subjects that completed at least one phone survey" (Results, p.3), so 2 of 16
  never completed a single survey. 5 relapses (hospitalisation or increased level of care).
- **Duration:** "up to 3 months"; "90-day study period"; clinic visits every 30 days.
- **Deployment model:** BYOD, Android and iOS. Confounder-minimisation stated: no "payments tied to
  use of the app, check-in calls or coaching around the app, providing subjects with new phones or
  study phones" (Discussion, p.7).
- **Configuration (Results p.2; Methods p.8):** accelerometer 10 Hz, 60 s on / 60 s off; GPS 1 Hz,
  60 s on / 600 s off; surveys at 10 a.m. every Monday, Wednesday, Friday, 23 questions across mood,
  anxiety, sleep, psychosis, medication adherence; passive streams GPS, accelerometer, anonymised
  call/text logs, screen on/off, charging status.
- **Survey adherence (with definition):** "of the 14 subjects that completed at least one phone
  survey, 12/14 (86%) completed more than two of the three surveys administered per week on
  average" (Results, p.3). Time from viewing to completing a survey "appears to decrease
  significantly over time across all patients".
- **Passive completeness (with definition):** coverage = observed pings / expected pings from the
  configured burst schedule (Algorithm 1, THRESHOLD 30 s). "The mean coverage of GPS and
  accelerometer data for patients within the first month of follow-up was 50.2 and 46.9%,
  respectively" (Discussion, p.5). GPS "of lower coverage than for accelerometer" in bursts,
  frequency and duration (Results, p.2). Within-burst accelerometer frequency "varies widely by
  patient, which may depend on the make and type of each user's phone". "data quality generally
  depends little on length of follow-up" (Results, p.3).
- **OS breakdown (Results p.4 / Discussion p.7; counts per OS not printed, in Supplementary
  Section 4):** "data from patients with Android phones exhibits significantly less GPS coverage
  recorded compared to data from patients with iOS phones, yet accelerometer data coverage did not
  significantly differ"; "iPhones had less accelerometer coverage and more GPS coverage compared to
  Android phones"; "Android users were more prompt in beginning and completing their surveys."
- **Pre-relapse pattern (Results p.4):** one participant relapsing around week 8 "ceased responding
  to any surveys in the 3 weeks prior to relapse despite nearly complete passive data coverage as
  recorded by GPS and accelerometer."
- **Technical failure modes / explanations offered (Introduction p.1; Discussion p.6–7):** subject
  turning off GPS; GPS sensor answering only a subset of queries; sampling shorter than requested
  or at a different frequency; GPS failing indoors; phone entering an inactivity mode in which
  passive sensors are not recorded; possible accelerometer suppression when the phone is still;
  OS/model reacting differently to apps that record and upload large data volumes.
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions:** "total coverage of passive data is moderately less than
  expected ... survey timing metrics vary greatly between subjects" (Discussion, p.5); "50%
  coverage might be sufficient to provide clinical insight" for sedentariness/sociability
  questions; metadata (coverage, latency, completion) is "not independent of patient-reported survey
  scores" and may be an early relapse signal; data quality and non-random missingness "need careful
  consideration in digital phenotyping". Recommendation: report and model data quality; replication
  via the released config file.
- **Not reported:** approached/refused; per-OS N (supplement only); 3-month retention; call/text
  log completeness; battery complaints; reasons the 2 subjects never took a survey.

### Suggested profile shape for the cohort (for the writing pass, not this screen)

Slug e.g. `beiwe-schizophrenia-state-clinic-pilot.md`. Quick Facts: 17 enrolled / 16 with data /
15 analysed for anomaly detection / 14 with any survey / 13 with any PSQI; up to 90 days; BYOD iOS +
Android; zero engagement scaffolding (no app-use payment, no check-ins, no reminders beyond the
app), $25 per clinic visit + $25/month phone-use stipend not tied to use (Staples). Completeness:
GPS 50.2%, accelerometer 46.9% of scheduled pings in month 1 (Torous 2018); accelerometer
availability mean 46%, range 2.1–89.1% (Staples); EMA ~50% completed overall (Staples) vs 12/14
survey-takers averaging > 2 of 3 weekly surveys (Torous 2018) — two different denominators, record
both. Failure modes: Wi-Fi-only upload lost one participant entirely; two relapsers uninstalled 1–3
weeks before hospitalisation; Android less GPS coverage, iOS less accelerometer coverage. Contrast
with `beiwe-als-adherence.md` on the active-vs-passive-outlasting question. Within-cohort
inconsistency to flag: survey frequency stated as twice weekly (Barnett NPP) vs three times weekly
(Staples, Torous 2018). Evidence confidence: Verified for coverage/funnel figures; note that all four
papers are the platform developers' own reports.

## 8. Berry 2019 — Design and results of a smartphone-based digital phenotyping study in ALS

- **File:** `2019-berry-annclintranslneurol-design-results-smartphone.txt`
- **First author (byline):** James D. Berry. Senior author Onnela.
- **Year / journal:** 2019, *Annals of Clinical and Translational Neurology* 6(5):873–881
- **DOI:** 10.1002/acn3.770. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform per Methods:** Beiwe, BYOD. Quote: "We used Beiwe, a research platform for
  smartphone-based digital phenotyping, to collect active (self-report ALSFRS-R surveys and speech
  recordings) and passive (phone sensors and logs) data from patients with ALS for approximately
  24 weeks." Matches filename.
- **Verdict: BORDERLINE-reject — duplicate-cohort (probable).** This is, in all likelihood, the
  cohort Beukenhorst 2022 calls "Study 1" in `beiwe-als-adherence.md`: single-centre MGH
  Multidisciplinary ALS Clinic, 23 consented with one death before data collection began (Berry) vs
  N=22 with "one participant died" (Beukenhorst study 1); 2016–2018 enrolment (Berry) inside
  Beukenhorst's Jul 2016 – Mar 2021 window; identical GPS duty cycle (60 s on / 600 s off);
  identical active tasks (weekly ALSFRS-R, Bamboo-passage speech, cough); Berry's Discussion says
  "We have also incorporated Beiwe into an ongoing clinical trial of inosine" = Beukenhorst study 3.
  One discrepancy prevents certainty: Berry describes a ~24-week protocol with an opt-out at
  week 12; Beukenhorst lists study 1 as 12 weeks with 3 clinic visits (Berry also has 3 visits:
  baseline, wk 12, wk 24). Beukenhorst may have analysed the mandatory 12-week phase only. Flag
  as "probable, not verified".
  Why reject rather than include: Berry reports **no retention rate, no attrition beyond the one
  pre-enrolment death, no data-completeness figure** ("Some smartphone-based ALSFRS-R data were
  missing" is the whole of it), and no technical failure modes. Beukenhorst 2022 gives all of
  those for the same participants. What Berry adds is small and belongs as an addendum to
  `beiwe-als-adherence.md`, not a new profile:
  1. OS split: **18 iOS / 3 Android** of 21 with phone type recorded (Table 2) — sharper than
     Beukenhorst's pooled "60% iOS".
  2. A burden-driven protocol change: "Daily surveys were removed after the first 10 participants
     completed the study due to informal participant feedback suggesting the frequency was
     burdensome" (Methods, p.876). Original schedule: 3 random ALSFRS-R items on 4 days/week plus
     the full 12-item weekly survey.
  3. Speech-recording yield: pause-time trajectory reported for **11 participants** ("10 out of 11
     participants", Results p.878) out of 22 with data — the paper does not say why only 11; a
     designer would read this as roughly half yielding analysable weekly speech, but that is an
     inference, so record it as "11 of 22 analysed; reason not reported".
  4. Accelerometer duty cycle here is **10 s on / 10 s off** (Table 1), which differs from the
     schizophrenia cohort's 60/60 and is not stated in the Beukenhorst profile.
  5. Self-report ALSFRS-R runs ~5% (2.4 points) above clinic scores, with divergence below a total
     of 39 (1.75-point drop per clinic point) and item-level gaps on swallowing (+0.4) and
     dressing/hygiene (+0.8) — a measurement finding, not a deployment one, but relevant to anyone
     planning smartphone ALSFRS-R as a trial outcome. Sample-size result: 230 participants with
     monthly smartphone ALSFRS-R vs 200 weekly (assumptions on p.878).
- **Funding / COI:** Winthrop Family Fund for ALS Science (MGH) and NIH DP2-MH103909. "None
  declared." Onnela senior author.
- **Existing Module 3 profile covering same cohort:** `beiwe-als-adherence.md` (Beukenhorst 2022),
