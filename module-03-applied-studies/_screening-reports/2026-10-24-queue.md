## 1. Li et al. 2026, JMIR Formative Research

- Source read: Europe PMC full-text XML (PMC13652396), CC BY 4.0. XML-only.
- First author: Yuan Li (first contributor record; Li and Wang flagged as contributing equally). A JMIR editor (Mavragani A) is listed last. Year 2026. JMIR Form Res 10:e93019. DOI 10.2196/93019. PMID 42854191. PMCID PMC13652396 (from the XML).
- Deployed (Methods, Study Procedure): "Participant 1 wore an Apple Watch Series 5 and an OURA ring ( Figure 1 A). Participant 2 wore a Fitbit Charge 5 and an OURA ring for four months". All three are profiled in Module 1. Matches the queue.
- Verdict: BORDERLINE-reject, no-cohort. A two-person case report under CARE guidelines, volunteers chosen partly for "regular use of a wearable device", with missingness percentages that have no day denominators. The before-and-after missingness change is the one transferable figure, and it is an uncontrolled change in two people.
- Existing profile on this cohort: none.

Numbers that would matter if reconsidered (all from the XML)
- Study July to December 2023, University of Florida, IRB exempt ET00042583. Participants not compensated.
- Funding: "The authors declared no financial support was received for this work. All wearable devices used in the study were purchased independently by the research team using the principal investigator's startup fund." Conflicts of interest: "None declared."
- Export routes: Fitbit web interface, Oura web portal, "Health Export CSV" app for Apple Watch.
- Protocol refinement on 2023-09-15 added weekly data checkups and reminders.
- Missing data, participant 1 (Table 4): Oura 75.2% before, 9.4% after. Apple Watch 36.4% before, 14.1% after. Diary 69.4% before, 6.25% after.
- Missing data, participant 2 (Table 5): Oura 40.3% before, 25% after. Fitbit 65.3% before, 20.3% after. Diary 36.3% before, 0% after.
- Causes named in Discussion: "device charging burdens, data synchronization failures, and the daily adherence required for the sleep diary." The limitations say charging burden and sync issues "were not systematically collected".
- Inconsistency: Results give the Apple Watch TIB bias as 0.6 minutes, the abstract as -0.6 minutes.
- Not reported: the number of nights in each period, the missing-data definition, wear-time definition.

## 2. Hallihan et al. 2026, JMIR Formative Research

- Source read: Europe PMC full-text XML (PMC13637571), CC BY 4.0. XML-only.
- First author: Hagar Hallihan (first `contrib-type="author"` record; editor Law S typed separately). Year 2026. JMIR Form Res 10:e88846. DOI 10.2196/88846. PMID 42832266. PMCID PMC13637571 (from the XML).
- Deployed (Methods, Protocol): "Each participant in the intervention group was issued an Apple Watch and instructed to pair it with their personal phone. Participants were also required to pair their Apple Watches with the PI's ABSTAIN LAB Apple Team account". Matches the queue (Apple Watch).
- Verdict: INCLUDE. Feasibility is a primary aim and the paper reports a screening funnel, retention, session attendance, a defined prompt-adherence rule with its rate, and watch wear days. Thin because there is no technical-failure or data-completeness content.
- Existing profile on this cohort: none.
- Profile written: `profiles/apple-watch-pst-college-alcohol-use-pilot-rct.md`.

Extraction
- Design: two-arm pilot RCT, 1:1 block randomization, block size 4, REDCap; analyst blinded; no registration in text (Randomization).
- Funding/COI: BCBS Illinois Health Equity Pilot Program; NIAAA R00AA030665. Conflicts "None declared." Both arms compensated with an Apple Watch on completion (Ethical Consideration).
- Population: college students 18 to 25, AUDADIS-5 past-year AUD, alcohol on 2 or more days a week; UIC and Rush plus online ads; mean age 22.54 (SD 2.20); 13/26 female; 17/26 Asian (Table 1).
- Enrolled to analysed: 132 screened, 106 excluded, 26 enrolled (12 PST-APPLE, 14 control), 26 analysed (Feasibility Test). Limitations give "7 of 33 (21%) initially recruited participants opted not to proceed during the tele-orientation phase", which does not reconcile.
- Target sample 44 from a G*Power calculation; recruitment "more challenging than anticipated".
- Duration: 12 weeks; recruitment May to September 2024; postintervention questionnaires within 1 week (Outcome Measures); abstract says "follow-up assessments at 3 months".
- Deployment model: watch issued, own phone. Watch model and phone OS not reported.
- Retention: 100% (Results; Figure 1 caption).
- Adherence: 100% attendance at 6 counselor sessions; weeks 7 to 12, 91.7% (11/12) completed at least 70% of prompted tasks per week; wear 6.2 (SD 0.8) days a week; overall prompted-task engagement 82% (Feasibility, Acceptability, and Adherence).
- Acceptability: CCSS 4.38 (SD 1.05) intervention vs 3.85 (SD 1.09) control. Methods say the CCSS was given to the intervention group only.
- Technical failure modes: none reported.
- Authors' conclusions: feasible, acceptable, hypothesis generating; recommend attention-matched control, 6 to 12 month follow-up, porting to Fitbit and Samsung Galaxy Watch.
- Not reported: wear-day definition and window, HR/sleep/step completeness, sync or device problems, export mechanism through the Apple Team account, how custom prompts reached the watch, iPhone requirement as an eligibility filter, cash compensation.

## 3. Birditt et al. 2026, Frontiers in Dementia

- Source read: Europe PMC full-text XML (PMC13642210), CC BY 4.0.
- First author: Kira S. Birditt (first author contributor record). Year 2026. Front Dement 5:1861809. DOI 10.3389/frdem.2026.1861809. PMCID PMC13642210 (from the XML).
- Deployed (Procedure): "Both the caregiver and care recipient wore Fitbit® Inspire devices for 2 weeks (data from which we plan to analyze in a future manuscript)." The measured instrument in this paper is the Roadmap positive-activity app and its nightly surveys. Roadmap is not a Module 2 platform; it is an intervention app.
- Verdict: REJECT, no-cohort. The only Fitbit deployment content is participation (20 of 20 caregivers, 15 of 20 recipients) and end-of-study acceptability items. No wear time, completeness or failure content for the profiled device. The queue's "100%/75% Fitbit wear" is participation, not wear.
- Existing profile on this cohort: none (We-Care, drawn from the SWEL-Care sample).
- Numbers that would matter if the Fitbit paper appears: 46 invited, 20 caregivers took part; of the 5 non-participating recipients, 2 lacked a compatible phone, 1 was nonverbal, 1 declined, 1 consented but disliked wearing the Fitbit. Fitbit acceptability 89.8%; "easy to sync" 94.4% (17). Paid $2 per day of Fitbit data; participants kept the Fitbit. Funding NIA P30 AG012846 and others; COI statement declares no commercial or financial relationships.
- Lead: watch for the planned Fitbit analysis from the same 14-day We-Care deployment.
