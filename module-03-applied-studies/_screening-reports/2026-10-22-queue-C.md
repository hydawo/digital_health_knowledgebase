## 1. Haimovich et al. 2026, JMIR Formative Research

- Full text: Europe PMC JATS (PMC13626187) and publisher PDF via Europe PMC `api/getPdf`. Stored at `literature/2026-haimovich-jmirformres-oura-ring-sleep-older-adults-ed-admission.pdf` (magic bytes %PDF-, pdftotext 4,752 words).
- First author (byline): Adrian D Haimovich (Haimovich, Lacourciere and Bertisch carry an asterisk). Year 2026. JMIR Form Res 10:e92359. DOI 10.2196/92359. PMCID PMC13626187 (PMID 42814730).
- Deployed (Methods, "Oura Ring Measures"): "Participants were provided an Oura Ring Gen 3 ... Each ring was synced directly to an Apple iPhone SE provided by the study team ... Data were exported from the Oura Teams platform as comma-separated value files." Matches the queue's guess.
- Verdict: INCLUDE. Recruitment funnel with reasons, MET-derived wear percentage, delirium-linked losses, an ICU device removal and an acceptability item.
- Existing Module 3 profile covering this cohort: none.

Extraction

- Design: single-site prospective cohort pilot, no sample size calculation, not registered (Methods, Data Analysis; Trial Registration).
- Funding/COI: NIA 1K23AG090744, R33AG058926 (GEAR), K24AG070106, R01AG030618. Oura "was not involved in study funding, design, data collection, analysis, interpretation, manuscript preparation, or the decision to submit." Unrelated author disclosures. Compensation not reported.
- Population: BIDMC ED, Boston. Age 65 or over, inpatient medical bed request 1 PM to 10 PM. Median age 78 (IQR 75 to 82, range 65 to 92). 7 of 14 female. 13 White, 1 Black (Table 1).
- Funnel (Results, Participant Flow): 29 approached. 9 declined, 2 lacked capacity with LAR declining, 3 lacked capacity with no LAR. 15 enrolled (2 by LAR). 1 withdrew immediately. 14 analysed. Screened count not reported.
- Duration: up to 4 hospital days or discharge. February to March 2025. Median 2 nights (IQR 1 to 3).
- Deployment model: provisioned ring and phone, returned at end.
- Wear definition (Data Analysis): minute worn if MET 0.9 or more; cessation is the first minute after which all MET values are below 0.9; percentage within complete 9 PM to 9 PM intervals, averaged. Criterion more than 75%.
- Completeness: 13 of 14 met the criterion (92.9%, 95% CI 66.1% to 99.8%). Median wear 93.4% (IQR 91.0% to 96.2%). The non-adherent participant was delirious at enrolment, about 8% wear (Results, Feasibility and Acceptability).
- Acceptability: 9 of 11 (81.8%, 95% CI 48.2% to 97.7%). Split 5, 4, 1 neutral, 1 strongly agree. 3 missed the exit survey through delirium. One was moved to ICU and the ring removed.
- Technical failures: ICU removal only. ED and ward time could not be separated (no location timestamps). Battery, charging and sync not reported. No OS breakdown (single study phone).
- Authors' conclusions: feasible and acceptable; delirium "may complicate device and study retention"; recommend multisite, location-aware monitoring, a PSG or research-actigraphy validation subset, and retention strategies for delirium.
- Not reported: screened count, reason for the immediate withdrawal, number discharged before day 4, compensation, charging protocol.

## 2. Boyle et al. 2026, JMIR Nursing

- Full text: Europe PMC JATS (PMC13524361) and publisher PDF via Europe PMC `api/getPdf`. Stored at `literature/2026-boyle-jmirnursing-garmin-somnofy-nursing-home-dementia-one-year.pdf` (%PDF-, 8,384 words).
- First author (byline): Lydia D Boyle. Year 2026. JMIR Nursing 9:e95194. DOI 10.2196/95194. PMCID PMC13524361 (PMID 42684378).
- Deployed (Methods, Sensing Technologies): "Garmin Vivoactive 5 was used for data collection at baseline and 6 months and upgraded to Garmin Venu3 at the 1-year time point for improved battery capacity and data storage capabilities." Data "retrieved using a hub through Fitrockr." Somnofy radar ran alongside and supplied all sleep metrics.
- Verdict: BORDERLINE-include. The deployment content is thin (no screening funnel, no support model). But it gives wear-time adherence at three timepoints over a year, named causes of missing data, deaths as the only attrition and a mid-study device change for battery and storage, in a population no other profile covers. Profiled on the Garmin deployment only.
- Existing Module 3 profile covering this cohort: none. Two earlier papers on the same DIPH.DEM cohort exist (Sensors 2025, DOI 10.3390/s25216635; Front Aging 2026, DOI 10.3389/fragi.2026.1768935). Neither is in the ledger or profiled.
- Expansion flag: Vital Things Somnofy (contactless radar sleep sensor) is a Module 1 expansion candidate. Fitrockr appears as the data broker; it is already on ROADMAP.md's batch-3 list.

Extraction

- Design: observational pilot; DIPH.DEM, pilot for 5-D (Design and Setting).
- Funding/COI: Helse Vest RHF F-12829-D10484. COI "None declared." Compensation and staffing not reported.
- Population: two dementia wards, one Norwegian nursing home. Age 77 to 93, mean 84.0. 8 F, 3 M. CDR mean 2.2 (Table 1).
- Funnel (Results): 11 recruited; 2 died; 9 analysed. Screened or eligible counts not reported.
- Duration: 7 days continuous wear at baseline, 6 months, 1 year; February 2024 to May 2025.
- Wear definition and rates (Results): total Garmin wear time after removing first and last days. 96%, 88%, 94%.
- Missing data causes (Results): charging 1 to 2 hours on day 4; participant removal for 2 hours or less.
- Data volume: 135 acceleration acquisition periods; 189 sleep periods over 219 nights (radar).
- Technical: model switch at 1 year for battery and storage; raw accelerometry at 25 Hz; no adverse events.
- Authors' conclusions: adherence and acceptability high; wrist ENMO poor longitudinally (ICC 0.00 to 0.02) but moderate to good cross-sectionally (0.58 to 0.79).
- Not reported: separate acceptability instrument (the abstract's 88% to 96% are wear figures), who charged the watches, per-participant missingness, sync or broker failures.

## 3. Bo et al. 2026, Experimental Gerontology

- Full text: medRxiv version (DOI 10.64898/2026.05.14.26353213, posted 2026-05-19, CC BY-NC 4.0), listed by Unpaywall as the accepted version. The Elsevier publisher version was not retrieved. Not stored, since rejected.
- First author (byline): Nannan Bo. Year 2026. Exp Gerontol, DOI 10.1016/j.exger.2026.113337. PMCID not in PDF (PMID 42785603).
- Deployed (Methods 2.1): "Step-count data were obtained from commercially available Garmin vívosmart® 5 devices ... retrieved via the Pattern Health platform." Matches the queue's guess at device level.
- Verdict: BORDERLINE-reject, architecture. The paper is a processing SOP plus a regression-calibration analysis, and the STRRIDE-PD Reunion cohort serves as its worked example. Its only deployment content is 68 with data, 1 excluded for fewer than 4 valid days, a median 10 valid days (range 6 to 40) and one 300-minute irregular interval. It gives no funnel from Reunion invitees to device recipients and no attrition.
- Existing Module 3 profile covering this cohort: none.

## 4. Hartman et al. 2026, Academic Emergency Medicine

- Full text: not obtained. The Wiley page and PDF (Unpaywall lists bronze OA) sit behind a Cloudflare bot check, both by curl and in the browser. Not bypassed. No PMC copy.
- First author: Hartman CA per Europe PMC metadata (PMID 42775945). Byline not verified.
- A medRxiv preprint exists (Leroux A et al., DOI 10.1101/2024.10.24.24316067, 2024). It is a materially different version: a different first author, N=197 rather than 196, and 82% rather than 77% attempted. Its Methods name a study-provided Apple Watch Series 4 and iPhone 12 running Apple ResearchKit active tasks in one supervised ED session (GAPcare II, NCT04304495). It reports attempt and completion rates and a 7.2% technology-related incompletion rate. I did not profile from the preprint, because its numbers differ from the paper in the queue.
- Verdict: REJECT, unobtainable. On the preprint it would be BORDERLINE (Apple Watch is profiled, but this was a single in-ED session, not a longitudinal deployment). Re-screen if Hassan supplies the published PDF.
