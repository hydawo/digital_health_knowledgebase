Screened 2026-09-03 against `RUBRIC.md`, CLAUDE.md "Module 3", and README "Scope rules".
Every text file in `txt/` was read in full (pdftotext -layout). Existing profiles consulted for

## 1. Barnett 2020 — REJECT (architecture)

- **File:** `2020-barnett-journalamericanmedicalin-determining-sample-size-length-follow-up-smartphone-based.txt`
- **First author (byline):** Ian Barnett. Co-authors Torous, Reeder, Baker, Onnela.
- **Year / journal:** 2020, *J Am Med Inform Assoc* 27(12):1844–1849
- **DOI:** 10.1093/jamia/ocaa201. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform per Methods (quote):** "we pooled 2 digital phenotyping studies that utilize the same digital phenotyping platform, Beiwe. The first study utilized Beiwe for up to 3 months in 17 patients with schizophrenia and the second for up to 30 months in 15 patients with bipolar disorder." Matches filename/folder (Beiwe).
- **Verdict:** REJECT — **architecture**. A power/sample-size methods paper (change-point detection + GLMM simulations, R package `DPsamplesize`, Shiny calculator). The two cohorts supply only effect-size parameters; no recruitment, retention, completeness or technical content of their own.
- **Only deployment-relevant sentence:** "Within our data, the level of missingness in smartphone accelerometer and GPS data, averaged over several cohorts, increased 0.5%–1% per week" (p.1845). This is the same figure already profiled with full CIs in `beiwe-missing-data-sociodemographic.md` (Kiang 2021: +0.5%/wk accel, +0.9%/wk GPS). Nothing new for Module 3.
- **Existing profile covering same cohorts:** the schizophrenia cohort is Barnett 2018 (not profiled; Module 2 territory); the McLean bipolar cohort is Study B in Kiang 2021.
- **Note for Module 2:** worth cataloguing in the Beiwe literature library as a study-design tool if not already there.

## 2. Beukenhorst 2021 (Muscle & Nerve) — REJECT (duplicate-cohort)

- **File:** `2021-beukenhorst-musclenerve-smartphone-data-during-covid19-pandemic-can.txt`
- **First author (byline):** Anna L. Beukenhorst (Beukenhorst and Ella Collins contributed equally; Onnela and Berry co-senior).
- **Year / journal:** 2021, *Muscle & Nerve* 63:258–262 (Clinical Research Short Report)
- **DOI:** 10.1002/mus.27110. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform per Methods (quote):** "Participants installed the Beiwe smartphone app on their personal smartphones ... The smartphone app was configured to collect location data using the GPS sensor for 60 seconds every 10 minutes, as described elsewhere [ref 5, Berry 2019]." BYOD; Table 1: 7 iOS / 1 Android. Matches filename.
- **Cohort:** N=8, "participants in an ongoing study were selected if passive mobility data were available between February 13 and April 13, 2020", recruited from MGH and Washington University ALS clinics. That is the 52-week observational cohort (Study 2, N=49) already profiled in **`beiwe-als-adherence.md`** (Beukenhorst 2022; same sites, same 1-min-on/10-min-off GPS duty cycle, same Berry 2019 parent study).
- **Verdict:** REJECT — **duplicate-cohort**. Checked against the existing profile per the rubric: this paper adds **no new operational figures**. It reports no enrolment funnel, no retention, no completeness (the selection criterion "passive data available" is itself an unquantified completeness filter — how many of the 49 were excluded is not stated), no technical failure modes. The only operational content is one narrative sentence: "During start of the COVID-19 pandemic, when many clinic-based observational studies were halted, data collection through smartphones continued." That is worth one line in the existing ALS profile, not a new profile.
- **Scientific content (out of Module 3 scope):** interrupted time series on imputed GPS (Barnett–Onnela method); home time 19.4→23.7 h/day, distance 42→3.7 km/day after the 13 Mar 2020 state of emergency.
- **COI:** extensive pharma disclosures for Berry, Paganoni, Miller; Onnela cofounder of a digital-phenotyping company.
- **Recommendation:** add a one-line "pandemic continuity" cross-reference to `beiwe-als-adherence.md`; do not build.

## 4. Liu 2021 — REJECT (architecture)

- **File:** `2021-liu-journalamericanmedicalin-bidirectional-imputation-spatial-gps-trajectories-missingness.txt`
- **First author (byline):** Gang Liu (with Onnela).
- **Year / journal:** 2021, *J Am Med Inform Assoc* 28(8):1777–1784
- **DOI:** 10.1093/jamia/ocab069. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform per Methods:** a GPS imputation algorithm (sparse online Gaussian Process, now Forest `jasmine`). Real data used only for a computational-timing benchmark: "a dataset collected as part of an ongoing study at the Massachusetts General Hospital. The study sought to determine if smartphone GPS data could reveal differences in recovery after breast-conserving surgery and mastectomy" (i.e. Panda et al. 2021 *Ann Surg Oncol*, ref 18 — Beiwe). Ground-truth validation used a dedicated GPS logger worn by the author for 3 days, plus synthetic data.
- **Verdict:** REJECT — **architecture**. No cohort of its own; the MGH data are a timing input only.
- **Only deployment-relevant figures:** "The size of observed data in 1 week was approximately 5040 triplets ... and the missing data duration was 154 hours on average across all individuals in the cohort" — i.e. ~92% of wall-clock time unobserved, consistent with the Beiwe duty cycle plus non-collection; and the general statement that GPS "may be sampled only for a small portion of time, frequently <10%". Also the sampling recommendation that, with imputation, 3 min sampled per 15 min suffices for CDC-style colocation detection vs 12/15 without. These are design heuristics, not deployment results.
- **Flag for Module 3 discovery:** the MGH breast-surgery Beiwe cohort (Panda et al. *Ann Surg Oncol* 2021;28:985–994, and Panda et al. *JAMA Surg* 2020;155:123–129) is **not profiled** and is the parent deployment of paper 6 below.

## 6. Panda 2021 (Annals of Surgery Open) — BORDERLINE-include

- **File:** `2021-panda-annalssurgeryopenperspec-expected-versus-experienced-health-related-quality-life.txt`
- **First author (byline):** Nikhil Panda (Panda and Ian Solsky co-first; Cauley and Haynes co-senior; Onnela co-author).
- **Year / journal:** 2021, *Annals of Surgery Open* 2:e060
- **DOI:** 10.1097/AS9.0000000000000060. **PMCID:** not in PDF. Open access (CC-BY).
- **Platform per Methods (quote):** "Consenting patients were instructed to download the Beiwe smartphone application, the user-facing component of a platform designed and previously used by our team for smartphone-based digital phenotyping research in surgical patients." BYOD; Table 1: 61 iPhone (82.4%) / 13 Android (17.6%) of the 74 analysed. **Surveys only in this analysis** (custom expectations survey + SF-36 via Beiwe); no passive data are reported here, although the parent cohort collected GPS/accelerometer (refs 6–8). REDCap held EHR-abstracted covariates. Matches filename.
- **Existing profile covering same cohort:** **none.** The `surg` grep hit spine-disease (Cote 2019, neurosurgery clinic), Pain-IDR, Samsung palliative and Withings post-op only. This is the MGH cancer-surgery Beiwe cohort (2017–2019) whose primary deployment papers are Panda 2020 *JAMA Surg* and Panda 2021 *Ann Surg Oncol* (both unprofiled; the latter is the dataset in paper 4).
- **Verdict:** **BORDERLINE-include.** Why include: it carries a defined, per-timepoint survey-retention funnel with an explicit AAPOR completion rule, a completer/non-completer baseline comparison, an OS breakdown at every timepoint, and a missing-data sensitivity analysis — more than "N enrolled, N analysed". Why borderline: it is survey-only, reports **no** reasons for non-response, **no** technical failure modes, **no** passive-data completeness, and its feasibility discussion is one sentence in Limitations. Given Beiwe's 12/55 share, the right move is **one cohort profile built primarily from the parent papers (JAMA Surg 2020 / Ann Surg Oncol 2021), with this paper as the survey-retention source** — not a standalone profile of this paper. If the parent papers prove unobtainable, this paper alone is thin but usable.

### Extraction (page/section references to the published PDF)

- **Design:** prospective cohort, single academic cancer centre, July 2017–July 2019 (Methods, p.2).
- **Funding/COI:** NIH T32 DK007754-18 (Panda); Ariadne Labs Spark Grant (Paul G. Allen Family Foundation); NIH/NIMH 1DP2MH103909 (Onnela). Onnela discloses Otsuka and Mindstrong Health funding; Panda a DARPA-contractor agreement (Disclosure box, p.1).
- **Population/setting/country:** adults ≥18 scheduled for breast, skin/soft-tissue, head-and-neck or abdominal tumour surgery; USA (MGH, Boston). Analysed n=74: mean age 53.9 (SD 13.9), 66.2% female, 86.5% non-Hispanic White, BMI 27.8; breast 33.8%, abdominal 31.1%, skin/soft tissue 20.3%, head and neck 14.9% (Table 1, p.4).
- **Eligibility exclusions relevant to deployment:** "patients were excluded if they did not own a smartphone or were not fluent English-speakers" (Methods, p.2). **Number screened out for no smartphone: not reported.**
- **Enrolled → analysed (Fig. 1, p.3; Results, p.2):**
  - Consented: **101**
  - Completed both preoperative surveys (expectations + SF-36): **74 (73%)** — the analysed sample
  - Excluded 27 for not completing preoperative surveys
  - Postoperative SF-36 completed: **1 month 42 (42%)**, **3 months 33 (33%)**, **6 months 24 (24%)** (denominators are the 101 consented, per Results text "42 (42%) patients").
- **Completion definition (Methods, p.2):** AAPOR Standard Definitions; "Only surveys initiated within 1 week of the assigned date and those with ≥75% completion were included in the final analyses."
- **Duration:** 6 months post-op per participant; approached "approximately 1 week before surgery".
- **Deployment model:** BYOD (own smartphone required); surveys "electronically sent ... via the Beiwe smartphone application". Compensation: **not reported.** Reminders/contact strategy: **not reported.**
- **Custom integration:** REDCap for EHR-abstracted data; Beiwe for surveys; no middleware described.
- **Recruitment method:** approached after surgical consultation ~1 week pre-op (Methods, p.2). Approached-to-consented funnel: **not reported.**
- **Retention definition and rate:** survey completion at each timepoint per the AAPOR rule above; 42%/33%/24% at 1/3/6 months of consented.
- **Attrition reasons:** **not reported.** Completers vs non-completers of the preoperative surveys did not differ on age (53.9 vs 48.9, p=0.092), sex, race, BMI, OS (iPhone 82.4% vs 81.5%, p=0.771), tumour site, or neoadjuvant treatment (Table 2, p.4).
- **OS breakdown by timepoint (Table 3, p.5):** iPhone/Android = pre-op 61/13; **month 1: 42/0**; month 3: 32/1; month 6: 23/1. **Every one of the 13 Android users failed the 1-month SF-36 completion rule; only one Android user completed at 3 and 6 months.** The authors do not comment on this. Whether it reflects Android notification/delivery behaviour, an app issue, or chance with n=13 is not examinable from the paper — but it is the single most operationally important number here and should be recorded as an unexplained OS effect (Beiwe 2017–2019, pre-heartbeat).
- **Data completeness/missingness:** survey completion only, as above. **Passive-data completeness: not reported** (not analysed here). **Wear time: n/a.**
- **Technical failure modes:** **none reported.**
- **Missing-data handling:** repeated-measures linear mixed model with multiple imputation as a sensitivity analysis; observed vs predicted HRQL did not differ at 1, 3, 6 months (Table 5, p.7).
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions/recommendations:** Limitations only: "despite the relatively high completion rate of the preoperative surveys, there was loss to follow up when assessing experienced HRQL at 1, 3, and 6 months postoperatively" with no baseline differences between completers and non-completers and a reassuring sensitivity analysis (p.5). Also: "The wide ownership and usage of smartphones across social determinants of health underscores the possibility of performing studies with similar methods in a more generalizable patient population" (p.5) — asserted, not measured. No design recommendations.
- **Not reported (for the profile's "not reported" list):** approached/screened numbers; smartphone-ownership exclusions; compensation; reminder schedule; reasons for non-response; any technical issue; passive-stream completeness; whether Android non-completion was investigated.
- **Scientific result (context only):** preoperative expectations correlated poorly with experienced HRQL in 6/8 SF-36 domains; moderate in physical functioning (0.50) and physical role limitations (0.41).

## 8. Pellegrini 2022 — INCLUDE

- **File:** `2022-pellegrini-brainbehav-estimating-longitudinal-depressive-symptoms-smartphone-data.txt`
- **First author (byline):** Amelia M. Pellegrini (Pellegrini and Emily J. Huang joint first; Perlis and Onnela joint senior).
- **Year / journal:** *Brain and Behavior* 2022;12:e2077 (accepted Feb 2021)
- **DOI:** 10.1002/brb3.2077. **PMCID:** not in PDF. Open access (CC-BY).
- **Platform per Methods (quote):** "Participants installed the Beiwe application at the baseline visit ... accelerometer and GPS data from participants' smartphones were collected using Beiwe" (2.1, 2.5). BYOD, iOS and Android. Matches filename.
- **Cohort identification (per the batch note):** this is **neither** the Barnett 2018 Beth Israel schizophrenia relapse cohort (17 pts, 3 months) **nor** a student cohort. It is a distinct **MGH Center for Quantitative Health transdiagnostic outpatient cohort**, Partners IRB 2015P000666, recruited 2015–2018: 11 MDD, 11 bipolar, 11 schizophrenia/schizoaffective, 12 healthy controls. It is not one of Kiang 2021's six pooled studies (those were Harvard undergraduates, McLean at-risk, and NHS3). **No existing Module 3 profile covers it.**
- **Verdict:** **INCLUDE.** Meets (a) real 8-week deployment; (b) Beiwe; (c) CONSORT with itemised termination reasons, per-participant passive-completeness proportions with an explicit expected-minutes denominator, weekly survey completion trajectory, a pre-specified survey-adherence retention rule, compensation schedule, upload policy, and an explicit design recommendation. Its distinct contribution against the existing 12 Beiwe profiles: a **compensated, visit-supported, short clinical deployment where retention was high (84%) but passive completeness was poor and highly variable** — the cleanest illustration in the module that retention and data completeness are different outcomes. Also the only transdiagnostic psychiatric-outpatient Beiwe cohort. Value is moderate rather than high; the paper's primary aim is prediction, and the completeness detail sits partly in supplementary material not in the PDF.

### Extraction (section references to the published PDF)

- **Design:** prospective cohort, 8 weeks, five in-person visits (baseline + 4 biweekly follow-ups with rater-administered MADRS) (2.1).
- **Funding/COI:** NIMH/NHGRI 1P50MH106933 (Perlis); NIH 1DP2MH103909 (Huang, Staples, Lorme, Onnela). COI: Staples employed by Mindstrong Health; Onnela received an unrestricted gift from Mindstrong and research funding from Otsuka and Apple; Perlis multiple industry roles (Conflict of Interest, p.10).
- **Population/setting/country:** adult outpatients from MGH clinics (Boston, USA) plus advertised healthy controls, 2015–2018. Analysed n=41: 63% female, mean age 43 (SD 12, range 21–68), 71% White, 20% African-American, 7% Asian; baseline MADRS: HC 0.7, MDD 20.0, BD 9.7, SZ 6.2 (Table 1).
- **Eligibility:** ≥18, "owned a smartphone running an iOS or Android operating system and were judged likely able to comply with study procedures by the site investigator's estimation" (2.1). Smartphone-ownership screen-outs: **not reported.**
- **Enrolled → analysed (Fig. 1 CONSORT, p.5; Results 3.1):**
  - Consented and completed baseline: **45**
  - Terminated before first follow-up: **4** — unable to download the app (1), unable to use the app (1), declined to continue (2) → excluded from analysis
  - Included in analysis: **41**
  - Terminated after first follow-up: **3** — inconsistent access to mobile phone (1), declined (1), duplicate/already completed study (1)
  - Completed 8 weeks: **38 (84% of 45)**; final groups MDD 9, BD 9, SZ 9, HC 11.
  - **Two of the seven losses (4.4% of consented) were app-usability failures at onboarding; one more was phone-access.**
- **Duration:** 8 weeks per participant.
- **Deployment model:** BYOD. App installed and activated by study staff at baseline with a random 8-character Beiwe ID (2.4).
- **Compensation (2.1):** $50 after baseline, $100 on completion; $25 extra (total $75) if withdrawn early; parking/travel reimbursed. The Discussion notes "the participant payment employed in this study precludes strong conclusions about acceptability, the high retention rate suggests that, with compensation, participants are willing to adopt this technology."
- **Retention rule (2.2):** "To remain enrolled in the study, participants were required to respond to the surveys at least five times a week" (the daily 4-item Likert survey). Whether anyone was removed under this rule: **not reported.**
- **Sampling configuration (2.5):** accelerometer 10 s on / 10 s off; GPS 2 min on / 10 min off (expected GPS coverage 1/6 of minutes). Android-only anonymised call logs.
- **Upload policy (2.4):** Wi-Fi only, "to avoid charges associated with uploading large volumes of data, roughly 1GB per subject-month"; data encrypted on phone, in transit, at rest; expunged from phone after upload.
- **Data completeness (3.2, Fig. S2a — S2 not in PDF):** defined at participant level as minutes of data collected ÷ minutes expected, over full study days (day after baseline to day before last visit). Accelerometer expected = all minutes; GPS expected = 1/6 of minutes. Proportions "ranging from 0 to 0.99 for accelerometer and from 0 to 0.87 for GPS." **Accelerometer: 23/41 (56%) had ≥0.5. GPS: 16/41 (39%) had ≥0.5.** "The quantity of collected data for iOS phones tended to be greater on average than for Android phones" (no figures). Totals: 674,969,086 accelerometer and 14,733,731 GPS measurements. Per-participant hourly availability heatmaps (Fig. S4) show one participant with "long gaps with no data" and one with "medium data quality periods interspersed with periods with no data"; authors note "the timing of the missing gaps may not be random."
- **Survey completion (3.2, Fig. S2b/S3):** weekly PHQ-8 completion **95% for the first survey, 80% for the last** (~2 months); **78% of participants completed PHQ-8 on ≥8 weeks**; late completions credited to the later week. Daily 4-item survey completion: **not reported.**
- **OS breakdown:** iOS > Android on passive volume (qualitative only); Android comm-log subset n=19 (7 HC, 12 patients). Exact iOS/Android counts: **not reported.**
- **Wear time:** n/a (phone carriage discussed as a confound: activity curves are affected by "how often each participant carried their phone").
- **Technical failure modes:** app could not be downloaded (1) or used (1) at onboarding; inconsistent phone access (1); otherwise unexplained per-participant gaps in passive streams. No server/sync/battery incidents reported.
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions/recommendations (Discussion, p.8–9):** retention "suggests that, with compensation, participants are willing to adopt this technology"; passive predictors "may be inaccurate when the proportions of data collected are low"; "In future studies, strategies to reduce missing data (for example, by monitoring data missingness for each participant during the course of the study and intervening where required) merit consideration"; design "precludes conclusions about application of smartphone apps in longer-term studies or those using 'lighter touch' designs without in-person visits." Contrasts with Barnett 2018 where subjects "were not paid for app use, not given additional support ... and not provided with check-in calls."
- **Not reported:** screened/approached numbers; smartphone-ownership exclusions; exact OS split; daily-survey adherence; whether the ≥5/week rule was enforced; reasons for the passive gaps; per-group completeness; any pre/post-heartbeat relevance (data 2015–2018, pre-heartbeat — flag as lower bound as in the other Beiwe profiles).
- **Scientific result (context):** passive data did not improve MADRS prediction over baseline MADRS + demographics (RMSE 4.72 vs 4.66); PHQ-8 alone 4.27.
