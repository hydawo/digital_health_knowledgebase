## 1. `2022-mahalingaiah-ajog-apple-womens-health-study-design.txt`

- **First author (byline):** Shruthi Mahalingaiah. Senior authors B.A. Coull, R. Hauser, M.A. Williams (shared). Onnela is a co-author.
- **Year / journal:** 2022, *American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology* 226(4):545.e1–545.e29 (NIH author manuscript in PMC).
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ajog.2021.09.041. **PMCID:** not printed in PDF (header says "available in PMC 2023 September 25" only).
- **Platform/device actually deployed (Methods):** "The study is hosted within the Apple Research app (available on the Apple App Store)" (p.4); passive data from "certain iPhone sensors and from a paired optional Apple Watch. These include frequently visited locations with an anonymized identifier, watch-on-wrist state, and Optical Sensor (including accelerometer and heart rate)" (p.7, "Research sensor and usage data"); menstrual tracking read from HealthKit. → Module 1 **apple-watch-healthkit** (Apple Research app / HealthKit / SensorKit). Matches filename.
- **Verdict: INCLUDE.** Not a protocol-without-results: it reports the first 10,000 enrollees' onboarding funnel, six monthly survey response rates with denominators (Table 3), HealthKit tracking completeness, withdrawals, differential dropout, survey completion times, and the authors' retention conclusions. The sister paper to Truslow 2024 (same Research app, same launch, same eligibility template) — the two should cross-link.
- **Existing profile on same cohort:** none. `apple-heart-data-management-lessons.md` is the Stanford Apple Heart Study (Garcia 2022), a different study.

### Extraction
- **Design:** "mobile-application-based longitudinal cohort study involving survey and sensor-based data" (Abstract); descriptive report of the first 10,000 with ≥6 months follow-up; planned 10-year duration to Nov 2029, target 500,000 (p.5). IRB Advarra PRO00037562; NCT04196595.
- **Funding/COI:** "This study received funding from Apple, Inc. The funder had no role in the analysis and interpretation of data." Three authors (C.L.C., T.F.C., G.A.) are Apple Health employees and "own Apple, Inc stock." J.P.O. "is a cofounder of a recently established commercial entity that operates in digital phenotyping"; received an unrestricted gift from Mindstrong Health in 2018. NIEHS intramural support for four authors (Acknowledgments, p.13).
- **Population/setting/country:** US (all states + Puerto Rico), iPhone users (iPhone 6s+, iOS 13.2+), have menstruated at least once, ≥18 (19 AL/NE, 21 PR), English, sole user of iCloud/iPhone. Mean age 33.6 (SD 10.3); 69.1% White non-Hispanic, 12.0% Hispanic, 6.1% Black, 4.3% Asian; 51% college+; 70.3% employed; **72.2% Apple Watch users (7223/10,000)**; SES (MacArthur ladder) low 31.5% / middle 42.2% / high 25.4%; 96.1% identify as woman (Table 1, p.19–21). Baseline menstrual status: 87.6% menstruating, 6.3% menopausal, 1.8% lactating, 1.1% pregnant (Table 2).
- **Enrolled → analysed:** "there were 11,113 people who downloaded the Research app and clicked through to the AWHS, 10,459 who consented, and 10,030 who responded to the demographics survey. Eleven participants formally withdrew from the study within their first month" (Results, p.10; Figure 2). Analysis set = first 10,000 demographic responders enrolled 14 Nov 2019–20 May 2020. "On average, 370 participants enrolled per week." 429 enrolled in the same window but did not answer demographics (Suppl. Table 7): vs responders, more from the South (40% vs 35%), fewer Apple Watch users (45% vs 72%), fewer SensorKit heart-rate opt-ins (5% vs 24%) (p.11). Discussion: "Among the 10,502 participants who downloaded the app and were eligible, 96% enrolled and responded to the demographics section" (p.12).
- **Duration:** ≥6 months follow-up for all in this report; monthly surveys through Nov 2020.
- **Deployment model:** BYOD (own iPhone, optional own Apple Watch). No provisioned hardware. **"Incentive: There was no compensation for study participation."** (p.5). Reconsent every 2 years.
- **Custom integration:** Apple Research app (shared with Apple Heart & Movement and Apple Hearing studies); survey drafts reviewed by Apple "to conform to their design standards across the 3 studies" (p.4); data "stored securely in a system within Apple that is designed to meet the technical safeguard requirements of HIPAA"; identifiable data restricted to PI staff (p.5).
- **Recruitment method:** public announcement of Research app 10 Sep 2019; launch 14 Nov 2019; Harvard Chan study website; social media accounts created Mar–Aug 2020 posting 1–3×/week; podcast, media articles, press interviews (p.5–6). Discussion: "recruitment efforts were generally not targeted to specific populations but mainly comprised general media coverage and social media posts."
- **Retention definition and rates:** completion rate = "the proportion of individuals who have completed the survey among those eligible" for the Monthly Survey: Menstrual Update (MSMU); non-participation = no response; eligible excludes those reporting menopause/pregnancy/invalid at baseline or earlier month, plus withdrawals (p.8, Table 3 footnotes). **Table 3 (p.22):** baseline 9751/10,000 (97.5%); MSMU#1 5748/9238 (62.2%); #2 4413/9145 (48.3%); #3 3902/9091 (42.9%); #4 3752/9045 (41.5%); #5 3437/9008 (38.2%); **#6 3099/8972 (34.5%)**. "Among the first 10,000 enrollees, 6519 participated in at least 1 of the MSMU data collection (65.2%)" (p.11). Abstract: "38% (3490/9238) did not respond to the Monthly Survey: Menstrual Update after enrollment." Consecutive responses not required. Respondents censored at pregnancy and menopause.
- **Attrition reasons:** not reported beyond 11 formal withdrawals (reasons not given) and ineligibility (cumulative 1028 by survey 6, Table 2). "Non-White participants were slightly more likely to drop out than White participants"; month-6 responders "more likely to be educated, White, and unmarried"; Apple Watch use 82.5% among month-6 responders vs 72.2% at baseline (Table 1).
- **Data completeness (passive/HealthKit):** "Menstrual tracking data in HealthKit (either retroactive data or data collected during study period) were available for 82.7% of the women, and SensorKit data on heart rate were available for 24% of the women" (p.10; Table 1: SensorKit HR opt-in 24.4% (2438), 31.2% among month-6 responders). 72.4% tracked ≥1 bleeding event within 6 months of enrollment; 70.6% within 3 months; among those tracking, mean 4.44 calendar months (25–75%: 3–6) tracked within 6 months (max possible 7); among month-6 MSMU completers 95.1% ever tracked, 91.4% within 6 months, mean 5.29 months (Table 1). Definition: "months of contributed cycle tracking data where a logged bleeding event in the HealthKit was attributed to the calendar month of that bleeding event" (p.7).
- **Wear-time definition:** not reported (watch-on-wrist state is collected but no wear statistics are given).
- **OS breakdown:** iOS only by design.
- **Survey burden actually observed:** median 1.83 min for demographics (IQR 1.43–2.45) and 0.77 min for MSMU (IQR 0.55–1.10) vs Apple's usability estimate of 25 min enrollment / 5 min monthly (p.11; "survey completion times were considerably shorter than expected", p.12).
- **Technical failure modes:** **not reported** (no sync, upload, app-crash, or watch-pairing failures documented). Only design constraints: MSMU available for 1 week after delivery then expires; surveys before 4 weeks post-enrollment excluded from counts (p.7).
- **Missing-data handling:** none for this descriptive report; power calculation assumed 20% loss to follow-up and 90–95% incomplete survey response over 12 cycles (p.9). Authors note "Further evaluation of the data must be conducted to understand whether nonrandom dropout may bias the longitudinal effect estimates."
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions/recommendations:** "the loss to follow-up measured by the response rates for the MSMU is higher than other retention metrics such as tracked menstrual bleeding events in HealthKit"; retention efforts "utilizing education, communication, and engagement through a study update feature within the app"; "engagement strategies may be constructed to limit the loss to follow-up of certain subpopulations"; passive HealthKit menstrual logging "will limit missingness and avoid reliance on survey responses" (p.13). Generalisability limited to iPhone users.
- **Not reported (for the profile's "not reported" line):** screened-but-ineligible counts by reason (Figure 2 categories not legible in text), withdrawal reasons, any Apple Watch wear-time or sensor-data completeness beyond the opt-in rate, technical failures, survey-level completion by device ownership, per-participant passive data volume, retention beyond 6 months.

## 3. `2024-truslow-npjdigitalmedicine-heart-movement-study-scale.txt`

- **First author (byline):** James Truslow (Brigham and Women's). Corresponding: Sumbul A. Desai (Apple), Calum A. MacRae (BWH).
- **Year / journal:** 2024, *npj Digital Medicine* 7:242 (Brief communication). **DOI:** 10.1038/s41746-024-01187-5. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform/device actually deployed (Methods):** "mobile application-based longitudinal cohort study… Participants are asked to complete a series of surveys and to consent to sharing data collected from their Health app (which uses the HealthKit framework) and sensor data obtained directly from iPhone and Apple Watch"; "An additional requirement for AH&MS includes use of an Apple Watch (Series 1 or later) paired with an iPhone at the time of enrollment" (Methods, p.5–6). → Module 1 **apple-watch-healthkit**. Matches filename.
- **Verdict: INCLUDE** (human pre-judged; confirmed). Reports a large-cohort engagement decay with explicit definitions, a withdrawal curve, survey response decay, a passive-data proxy for wear, and a follow-up-call funnel.
- **Existing profile on same cohort:** none. Cross-link to Mahalingaiah 2022 (sister study, same app) and `apple-heart-data-management-lessons.md` (different study, same theme of scale not surviving a protocol).

### Extraction
- **Design:** "mobile application-based longitudinal cohort study involving the collection of sensor, survey, and health data"; enrolment began 14 Nov 2019, goal up to 500,000; planned 5 years to Nov 2024; Advarra IRB PRO00036784; NCT04198194. **"There is no compensation for participation."** (Methods, p.5). This paper: participants enrolled 2019-11-14 to 2020-11-13, observed to 2021-11-13 (each ≥1 and ≤2 years).
- **Funding/COI:** "All of the authors of the manuscript are Apple employees or partially funded by Apple through the American Heart Association." (Competing interests, p.10–11). Sponsor is Apple Inc.; AHA partner. Data not publicly available.
- **Population/setting/country:** US iPhone + Apple Watch owners, English, ≥18 (19 AL/NE, 21 PR). Cohort n=82,809: median age 37.2 [IQR 29.4–47.3], mean 39.3 ± 13.1; **73.6% men / 74.4% male at birth**; 71.8% White, 11.3% Hispanic, 6.6% Asian, 4.3% Black; 61.7% college+; 80.5% employed; BMI 28.4 ± 6.5; 5.3% smokers (Table 1). Depression 26.0%, anxiety 24.1% self-reported (Table 2). "Reference demography": 74% male vs 49% US; 72% white vs 60%; college-educated 89% vs 62% (p.9).
- **Enrolled → analysed:** post-enrolment exclusions only: QA test accounts n=29; eligibility became ambiguous after enrolment (e.g. edited DOB/address) n=100; **did not complete Demographics survey n=1751** → **82,809** (Methods "Baseline Characteristics", p.7). The 1751 excluded were 3.2 years younger (95% CI 2.58–3.79). Screened/downloaded/consented counts **not reported**.
- **Withdrawals:** "2,684 participants (3.24% of the cohort) withdrew from the study within one year of enrollment. Among those who withdrew, 25% withdrew in their first 13 days, and 50% withdrew within 111 days. Among those who withdrew < 1% were automatic withdrawals triggered by an update to a participant's state of residence or date of birth" (p.7; Suppl. Fig 1).
- **Duration:** ≥1 year per participant in this analysis.
- **Deployment model:** BYOD (own iPhone + own Watch). Research app; participants can opt in/out of each data type at any time. Coded data, encrypted in transit/at rest, HIPAA-designed system; identifiable contact info held only by BWH staff via a workflow "inaccessible to Apple" (p.6).
- **Custom integration:** Apple Research app (shared with AWHS and Hearing Study); HealthKit; SensorKit (SK); FHIR clinical records via Health app; BWH staff phone follow-up for triggered events.
- **Recruitment:** IRB-approved study websites (Apple, AHA, BWH); AHA social media and email campaigns (Oct 2020); in-app "updates" feature launched Oct 2020 "to encourage continued participation… and increase recruitment efforts" (p.6). Later "local and social media campaigns and friend or family member recruitment" to improve representativeness (p.5).
- **Retention / engagement definitions and rates (Fig. 1, "Measures of participation over time", p.8):** two indices, denominator always 82,809:
  1. Research app uploads any data to study servers on a given day (may be only a scheduled check-in — shows app installed and phone online).
  2. Watch shares a "Stand Hour" HealthKit sample — proxy for Watch wear ("If Watch is not on the wrist and powered on, then no sample is created"); authors note absence could also mean "the data upload path from Watch to the Research app and then to the AH&MS servers was not active (e.g. lack of connectivity)" or the participant opted out of sharing Stand Hours.
  - Daily non-participation: app non-upload ~1% at day 0 → **~38% at day 365**; Stand Hour non-sharing 5% at day 0 → **44% at day 365**.
  - Cumulative indefinite inactivity (dropout): app uploads <1% at day 0 → **28% at one year**; Stand Hours 2% → **34% at one year**.
  - Authors' reading: "the decline in daily participation over time is largely attributable to the accumulation of permanently inactive participants over time, and less attributable to a degradation of study engagement among active participants" (44% not sharing on day 365 vs 34% permanently stopped).
- **Survey engagement (Table 5, Fig. 2):** ≥1 submission in first year — Demographics 100%; Risk of Falling 85.2%; Mobility 80.8%; Stress Scale 80.4%; Mental Health 80.0%; Medical History 78.9%; Physical Activity 78.0%; Health Behaviors 75.1%; Medications 75.1%; Disability 72.8%; Changes in Health 67.9%; triggered surveys 0.6–32.6%. "almost all the surveys have a participation rate greater than 70%". Monthly Stress Scale response rate (42,181-person sub-cohort enrolled after 2020-05-01): **69.55% → 32.48% after one year**; quarterly Changes in Health (whole cohort, 400-day window): **60.69% → 34.06%** (p.8–9). Surveys expire 28 days after delivery (Demographics never expires). Estimated burden ~30 min first month, ~10 min/month thereafter.
- **Passive data completeness (single representative week 2021-11-07 to -13, Table 3/Suppl. Table 8):** step count, heart rate and stand hours shared "by about half of the cohort"; 25,304 (30.6%) shared ≥1 workout (mean 6.54); Mindfulness 5.5%; high heart-rate event 2.4%; blood glucose (third-party) 1.1%. Week validated as typical vs 145 other weeks (Mahalanobis d² = 1.57, p = 0.954). In a week the cohort contributed ~137,000,000 heart-rate samples, ~57,800 VO2max estimates.
- **ECG:** 66,752 (80.6%) had an ECG-capable Watch (Series 4–6) for ≥1 day in year 1; 16,051 had Series 1–3/SE; **55,740 (83.5% of capable) recorded ≥1 ECG; 1,132,473 ECGs; median 8 per user**; 25,402 (2.2%) classified AF in 1,641 participants (2.0%), of whom 477 (29.1%) already knew they had AF (Table 4, p.3, p.8). Encouraged by an enrolment task and a bi-weekly task Apr 2021–Jan 2022.
- **Clinical records (FHIR):** "the proportion of participants who have been able to share these data types is modest (~10%) as a consequence of local FHIR compliance and the process required"; 8408 shared ≥1 record (7757 within year 1); "initial delays due to low healthcare utilization during COVID" (p.3, p.5, p.8).
- **Human follow-up funnel (Potential fall surveys, p.9):** 2055 survey responses meeting follow-up criteria (1735 participants) → consent to phone follow-up 1392 surveys (1179 participants) → **callers reached 967 (829 participants; 47% of eligible surveys, 48% of participants)**.
- **Wear-time definition:** Stand Hour presence per day as wear proxy; no hours/day wear statistics.
- **OS breakdown:** iOS only by design; Watch series breakdown as above.
- **Technical failure modes documented:** stated qualitatively in Limitations: "Other forms of missing data are prevalent… These challenges include timing of data loss with respect to phone upgrade, the characteristics of participation prior to study drop-out, frequency of contact, survey length, prior survey completion and many others"; possible upload-path inactivity (connectivity) as a cause of missing Stand Hours; FHIR sharing blocked by "local FHIR compliance and the process required" (p.5). Automatic withdrawal on eligibility edits (<1% of withdrawals). No quantified sync/battery/app-crash data.
- **Missing-data handling:** none in this descriptive paper; "this missingness must be accounted for in any analyses."
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions:** "The sustained survey engagement of participants is considerably improved from prior studies of serial health questionnaires"; "The loss to follow-up estimated from monthly survey response rates is higher than other retention metrics, though the survey characteristics and delivery cadences have not yet been optimized for response or retention rates"; "The patterns of dropout in the initial year of the study skew participation further in the direction of initial recruitment biases, emphasizing the need for systematic approaches to drive representativeness in participant recruitment and retention"; "Female study recruitment has steadily grown over time and retention of this demographic is high" (data not shown). Recommends dynamic recruitment strategies, rapidly modifiable app, participant annotation, consented EHR access (p.4–5).
- **Not reported:** download/consent funnel before enrolment; wear hours per day; per-participant passive data days; reasons for withdrawal; sex-stratified retention numbers ("data not shown"); Watch-model effects on engagement; any device or app failure counts; representativeness over time ("data not shown").

## 4. `2025-hu-natscisleep-quantification-differences-sleep-measurement-wrist-worn-consumer.txt`

- **First author (byline):** Cindy R Hu. Co-authors include Onnela, Coull, Redline, James, Hart.
- **Year / journal:** 2025, *Nature and Science of Sleep* 17:1973–1983. **DOI:** 10.2147/NSS.S530812. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform/device actually deployed (Methods):** "participants were asked to wear a research-grade accelerometer and a consumer wearable device concurrently on their wrist for fourteen consecutive days and to complete daily sleep logs on their smartphone using a research application called Beiwe"; "GT3X+ and Actisleep models from Actigraph… 60-second epochs… 30 Hertz"; "Participants wore a Fitbit… Charge 3 and Charge 5… Sleep data were synced to and downloaded from Fitabase" (p.1974–1976). → Module 1 **fitbit-google** + **ametris-actigraph**; Module 2 **beiwe** (sleep diary only). Filename implies a validation paper, which it is; the Beiwe/ActiGraph components are not in the filename.
- **Verdict: BORDERLINE-reject — validation.** The paper's object is Fitbit sleep accuracy against actigraphy + diary (TST bias −16.0 min, MAPE 11.18%, ICC 0.79; SE ICC 0.39). It does, however, print one genuinely operational funnel for a 14-day three-instrument free-living deployment: **63 kits sent → 12 excluded for no days with all three data streams (9 "device (ie, research-grade accelerometer, consumer wearable, or smartphone application) issues", 2 protocol non-adherence [wearing devices on different wrists], 1 never returned the accelerometer) → 47 analysed** (p.1974, Figure 1); mean 12.5 (SD 3.6) sleep periods of 14 days; Fitabase used "to monitor participants' data on a daily basis"; paper diary as backup for app issues; participants kept the Fitbit as compensation; eligibility required Wi-Fi ≥ weekly and ability to wear 24 h/day for 14 days. That is a 19% loss to device/app problems in two weeks, which a study designer would want — but the paper does not say which device failed, when, or why, and gives no wear-time or per-stream completeness. Recommend: catalogue in Module 1 (Fitbit + ActiGraph literature) and cite the 63→47 funnel from the Fitbit profile; not a standalone Module 3 profile. If the parent wants it included, it should be a short profile flagged "operational content = one paragraph".
- **Existing profile on same cohort:** none. `beiwe-nurses-health-study-burst.md` is NHSII (Yi 2025), not NHS3 SPAVS; same group (James, Onnela, Chavarro, Hu) — worth a cross-reference line either way.
- Funding NIH (U01 HL145386, R24 ES028521, P30 ES000002, F31 ES035252); "Brent Coull reports grants from Apple, Inc." Population: 47 female nurses, mean age 40.4 (SD 6.9), 95.7% White; Sept 2023–May 2024; contiguous US.

## 5. `2021-rahimieichi-jmirmhealthuhealth-open-source-longitudinal-sleep-analysis-accelerometer-data.txt`

  - Not reported: any enrolment funnel, retention definition, Beiwe passive completeness, OS split, wear-time per day.

## 6. `2024-straczkiewicz-ebiomedicine-upper-limb-movements-digital-biomarkers-people.txt`

- **First author (byline):** Marcin Straczkiewicz. Co-authors Karas, Johnson, Burke, Scheier, Royse, Calcagno, Clark, Iyer, Berry, Onnela.
- **Year / journal:** 2024, *eBioMedicine* 101:105036. **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ebiom.2024.105036. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform/device actually deployed (Methods):** "Twenty PALS wore a wearable accelerometer, ActiGraph Insight Watch, on their wrist for six months. They also used Beiwe, a smartphone application that collected self-entry ALS Functional Rating Scale—Revised (ALSFRS-RSE) survey responses every 1–4 weeks"; "raw sub-second level accelerometer data with a 32 Hz sampling rate… transferred to the ActiGraph CentrePoint Data Hub via Bluetooth and subsequently to the CentrePoint cloud using cellular data" (p.3). → Module 1 **ametris-actigraph**; Module 2 **beiwe** (survey delivery only). Matches filename.
- **Duplicate-cohort check:** this is the **ActiGraph arm of Johnson et al. 2023** (`beiwe-actigraph-modus-als-progression.md`): same site (MGH NCRI/Healey Center), same enrolment window (Jan–Dec 2021), same sponsor (MTPHA), same device configuration, same $50/3-months compensation, same "one was unable to download the application" event, and identical days-with-data median 178 [23, 191]. 23 enrolled here = the 23 who chose ActiGraph out of Johnson's 46. It is **not** the ARC/ALSTDI cohort in `actigraph-als-upper-limb-wear-time.md` (that is GT3X+, N=202, 2014–2023; this paper uses ten separate ALS patients in controlled conditions only for analytical validation of the metric).
- **Verdict: BORDERLINE-include — duplicate-cohort overlap, but it adds new operational numbers.** Recommend an addendum section in `beiwe-actigraph-modus-als-progression.md` (plus a ledger entry) rather than a new profile. The genuinely new figures:
  1. **A second valid-day definition applied to the same arm.** Johnson used ≥8 valid hours/day (vendor wear flag): median **158** valid days [21, 191]. This paper uses **≥21 h (1260 min) of wear by the Choi algorithm**: median **124** valid days (range 3–165), mean 97.4 (SD 51.9) (Table 2, p.6). Same participants, same raw data, 22% fewer valid days at the median and a minimum of 3 — a within-cohort demonstration of the threshold-sensitivity result already in the module from the ARC cohort, now on a consumer-cloud device. Wear compliance definition: "valid days divided by days in the observation period" (p.5).
  2. **OS split for this arm:** iOS 15 (75%) / Android 5 (25%) (Table 2) — Johnson gave 82.5% iOS for the pooled 40.
  3. **Survey cadence actually achieved:** surveys mean 6.9 (SD 3.6), median 6 [2, 15]; days between surveys mean 36.8 (SD 24.0), median 32 [6, 112] (Table 2) — the inter-survey interval spread was not in Johnson's profile.
  4. **Metadata gap:** "we did not prospectively collect information on patient handedness, though we retrospectively collected this from 17 out of 20 participants"; analysis used non-dominant wrist for 17 and left wrist for the 3 unknown (Limitations, p.10). Echoes the 84/438 handedness loss in the ARC profile.
  5. **Device constraint:** Insight Watch lacks gyroscope and magnetometer ("unavailable in the devices used in our study"), same limitation as GT3X+.
