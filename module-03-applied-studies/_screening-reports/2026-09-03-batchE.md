## 1. Vidal Bustamante 2022 — INCLUDE

- **File:** `2022-vidal-scientificreports-fluctuations-behavior-affect-college-students-measured.txt`
- **First author (byline):** Constanza M. Vidal Bustamante (co-first with Garth Coombs 3rd). Senior: Randy L. Buckner. Onnela is a co-author.
- **Year / journal:** 2022, *Scientific Reports* 12:1932
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41598-022-05331-7. **PMCID:** not in PDF (PMID 35121741 appears only in the 2024 paper's reference list).
- **Platform/device actually deployed (Methods, p.12):** "own an Apple iPhone or Android smartphone compatible with the smartphone application used to collect daily surveys, Beiwe" and "wearing an actigraphy wristband for continuous activity and sleep monitoring for the duration of the study (GENEActiv Original, Activinsights Ltd., Kimbolton, UK)". Beiwe = **surveys only** (no passive smartphone sensing reported). Wearable = **GENEActiv** (Module 1 profile `axivity-geneactiv`). Matches filename.
- **Verdict: INCLUDE.** Reason: a year-long, two-instrument (research-grade wrist actigraph + smartphone EMA) deployment with a stated recruitment funnel, per-stream observation counts, a documented actigraphy technical failure, an explicit device-swap logistics model, an incentive structure, and an 88% re-enrolment rate two years later. Beiwe is over-represented in Module 3, but the operational novelty here is the **GENEActiv** side: there is no GENEActiv/Axivity deployment in Module 3 yet, and this is the module's longest continuous wrist-actigraphy protocol on healthy young adults. Beiwe appears purely as the survey vehicle.
- **Existing profile covering the cohort:** none. Possible overlap with Kiang 2021 (`beiwe-missing-data-sociodemographic.md`), whose Studies A/C/E/F are "healthy Harvard College undergraduates" 2015–2018 with Baker/Buckner/Onnela as co-authors; Kiang pooled accelerometer/GPS *sensor metadata*, whereas this paper used Beiwe for surveys only, so the data are not the same stream even if the students overlap. Unverifiable from either text; flag in the profile.

### Extraction (INCLUDE)

- **Design:** intensive longitudinal observational, one full academic year "and a few days into the summer break" (Methods, p.12), plus a fully remote 13-week COVID-19 follow-up two years later (p.13).
- **Funding/COI:** gift from Kent and Liz Dauten; NIMH U01MH116925, DP2MH103909; NIH T90DA022759; Sackler; Harvard Foundations of Human Behavior (p.16). **COI:** "J.P.O. is a cofounder and board member of a commercial entity, Beiwe, established in 2020" (p.17); Baker: Verily consulting, Mindstrong consulting/equity.
- **Population/setting/country:** first-year students living on campus at "an elite university in the United States" (Harvard, per Fig.1 "Harvard-Yale"); ages 18–19, mean 18.06; 25 female of 49; 63% White, 14% Black, 10% Asian, 4% American Indian, 4% mixed; 12% prior psychiatric diagnosis (p.12). **94% iPhone / 6% Android** (p.12).
- **Enrolled → analysed (Methods, p.12, verbatim):** "From an initial recruitment pool of 68 individuals, 19 were excluded from analysis based on issues with data acquisition including early withdrawal from the study (n = 7), technical failure of the actigraphy data (n = 1), poor quality actigraphy data (n = 2), and completion of < 100 daily surveys across the data collection period (n = 9). The final cohort was N = 49." Retained fraction 49/68 = 72%.
- **Duration:** 256 days from first day of Fall semester to last day of Spring (p.14); actigraphy across the whole year including a 5-week winter break.
- **Deployment model:** BYOD smartphone for Beiwe; **researcher-provisioned GENEActiv wristbands**, swapped for "a fully charged one with reset memory at the in-person check-ins" every 3–4 weeks (p.12). Sampling rate deliberately dropped from 30 Hz on campus to **10 Hz over winter break** "in order to extend battery life and memory while participants were not on campus" (p.12): a documented protocol change driven by device constraints.
- **Custom integration:** DPSleep pipeline (Rahimi-Eichi 2021) for sleep detection with two-rater manual QC of every sleep episode (p.12); REDCap for questionnaires.
- **Recruitment method:** flyers and e-mail within the first two weeks of Fall semester; in-person consent session (p.12).
- **Incentives (p.12):** hourly pay for online surveys; **$1 per daily survey submitted; $1 per day of continuous wristband wear**; milestone bonus at half-study and a larger one at full completion. Follow-up study: $10 per biweekly survey, $1 per daily survey, milestone bonus "scaled to reward few missed surveys" (p.13).
- **Retention definition and rate:** in-study retention is not reported as a rate beyond the funnel. **Follow-up re-enrolment: 43/49 (88%), ">82% retention within each of the three student clusters"; 3 of the 43 provided only biweekly surveys**; the 6 non-enrolers (1 Cluster A, 3 B, 2 C) "declined to participate or did not respond to our contacts" (p.13).
- **Attrition reasons:** only the four exclusion categories above; no reasons for the 7 early withdrawals.
- **Data completeness (Table 1, p.3):** "Daily actigraphy: available observations per subject" range **132–249, mean 220, median 227** (of ~256 days); "Daily survey: available observations per subject" range **98–255, mean 202, median 216**. Survey on-time definition: submitted between 5 PM and 6 AM next day; later submissions "discarded and marked as missing" (p.13). Inclusion threshold: ≥100 on-time daily surveys. Fig.1/Fig.2 top panels plot the daily proportion of missing survey observations; days with >50% missing are shaded. Discussion (p.11): "there were non-trivial amounts of missing data, especially among the daily phone-based surveys toward the last few months of the study. Although missingness in the daily assessments were not associated with participants' global clinical scores…"
- **Wear-time definition:** DPSleep removes non-wear minutes by tri-axial variance; a day is "unusable" if either sleep-episode boundary cannot be detected (p.12). No hours-per-day threshold reported.
- **OS breakdown of completeness:** not reported (94% iPhone makes it moot).
- **Technical failure modes:** 1 participant excluded for "technical failure of the actigraphy data"; 2 for "poor quality actigraphy data" (p.12). The companion 2024 paper adds that one analysed participant (T15) "had no usable actigraphy data over the full spring semester due to a technical issue with their wristband" (2024, p.17). No phone/app/sync failures reported.
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions:** "Most enrolled participants were highly compliant, but there were non-trivial amounts of missing data… Payment structures and phone notifications were designed for participant compliance and retention, but future work should explore additional strategies" (p.11). Sample "might not be representative of all first-year college students" (p.12).
- **Not reported (for the profile's "not reported" lines):** screened-to-approached numbers; reasons for early withdrawal; per-participant wear hours/day; any sync/upload or app issues; whether the 9 low-survey participants were still wearing the wristband; battery life achieved per swap cycle; support/contact burden.

## 2. Karas 2024 — INCLUDE (overlapping cohort with Johnson 2023; adds the passive-stream figures that profile explicitly lacks)

- **File:** `2024-karas-annclintranslneurol-tracking-als-disease-progression-passively-collected.txt`
- **First author (byline):** Marta Karas (shared first with Julia Olsen); shared senior Berry and Onnela.
- **Year / journal:** 2024, *Annals of Clinical and Translational Neurology* 11(6):1380–1392
- **DOI:** 10.1002/acn3.52050. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform actually deployed (Methods, p.1382):** "Remote self-entry ALSFRS-R (ALSFRS-RSE)… and smartphone sensor data (accelerometer, GPS) were obtained using the Beiwe smartphone application (app)." Configuration: accelerometer 10 Hz, 10 s on / 10 s off; GPS 1 min on / 10 min off. **Beiwe, BYOD, passive + active.** Matches filename. No wearable.
- **Duplicate-cohort check.** Same centre (MGH), same sponsor (MTPHA), same recruitment sentence, same remote model, same 2–4-week ALSFRS-RSE cadence, overlapping authors (Johnson, Karas, Straczkiewicz, Burke, Iwasaki, Lahav, Scheier, Clark, Iyer, Berry, Onnela) as `beiwe-actigraph-modus-als-progression.md` (Johnson 2023: 46 enrolled Jan–Dec 2021, 6 months). Karas: **63 enrolled Jan 2021 – Jul 2022, up to 1 year, data pull 6 Sep 2022**. Neither paper cross-references the other as the same cohort, but the enrolment window and design make Johnson's 46 very likely a subset of Karas's 63. Not the Beukenhorst 2022 cohort (`beiwe-als-adherence.md`: 2016–2021, weekly surveys, three separate studies) and not the ARC/ActiGraph registry (`actigraph-als-upper-limb-wear-time.md`).
- **Why INCLUDE despite overlap:** the Johnson profile states in bold that it is "Not a Beiwe passive-sensing study… No smartphone GPS, accelerometry, or log data was collected" and that the heartbeat/background question "does not apply". Karas 2024 is the passive-sensing half of the same programme and supplies the numbers Johnson cannot: a five-step analytic funnel, per-participant valid accelerometer minutes/day, GPS collection minutes/day, an LMM showing valid minutes did **not** decay over a year, and a severity difference between included and excluded participants. Under the rubric's duplicate-cohort rule (new operational figures → include and note overlap), this qualifies. Recommend a profile explicitly cross-linked to Johnson 2023 as "the passive-stream companion".

### Extraction (INCLUDE)

- **Design:** single-centre, non-interventional, remote prospective observational, up to 1 year (p.1382).
- **Funding/COI (p.1390):** "financially supported in part by a grant from Mitsubishi Tanabe Pharma Holdings America, Inc. (MTPHA). Listed authors from MTPHA were involved in protocol development and manuscript review." Iwasaki is an MTPHA employee; Lahav a paid MTPHA strategic advisor; Berry reports MTPHA and other industry support. Onnela declares no competing interests (platform-developer COI unstated here, unlike Vidal 2022 which declares it).
- **Population:** ambulatory adults with ALS (El Escorial), "able to… use their smartphone independently" (p.1382). Analytic n=45: 64.4% male, mean age 60.1 (SD 10.7), 91.1% White, **82.2% iOS / 17.8% Android**, median baseline ALSFRS-RSE 37 [19–48], median 50 months since onset (Table 1, p.1384). USA.
- **Enrolled → analysed (Results, p.1383–1384, verbatim):** "Data from 63 PALS enrolled between January 2021 and July 2022 were considered… a subset of 54 PALS met the condition for having at least two ALSFRS-RSE surveys completed (57 had any), 52 had at least 28 days of valid accelerometer data, and 53 had at least 28 days of valid GPS data. Taking intersection of these subsets, the analytic sample consisted of 45 PALS." So 63 → 57 (any survey) → 54 (≥2 surveys) → 45 (all three criteria); 18 excluded (28.6%).
- **Selection effect on severity (p.1384):** excluded 18 had "higher mean age (63.2 years)… and lower median baseline ALSFRS-RSE score (32.5; calculated for 12 out of 18 PALS for whom it was available)" vs 37 in the analytic sample. A wear/validity criterion again selecting toward less-impaired participants (compare `actigraph-als-upper-limb-wear-time.md`).
- **Duration:** observation period mean **292.3 days (SD 87.4), median 291 [53, 398]** (Table 2, p.1385); end = 1 year, withdrawal, death, or 6 Sep 2022, whichever first.
- **Deployment model:** BYOD; "Participants were instructed on how to download and use the Beiwe app on their phones (Android or iOS) during the baseline visit procedures"; app removed at completion; "Participants were able to contact study coordinators for technical assistance, though **these calls were not systematically logged**" (p.1382). No compensation mentioned in this paper (Johnson reports $50 at 3 and 6 months for the wearable arm).
- **Custom integration:** Forest library (GPS imputation via sparse online Gaussian process; step counting); code at github.com/onnela-lab/als-beiwe-passive-data.
- **Recruitment:** "advertised on ALS social media accounts, on an institutional research recruitment website, and at the ALS multidisciplinary clinic" (p.1382). Screened-to-enrolled not reported.
- **Retention:** no dropout curve, no withdrawal/death counts. The only proxy is the observation-period distribution (min 53 days against a 365-day target).
- **Data completeness (Table 2, p.1385, mean (SD), median [min, max]):**
  - ALSFRS-RSE submissions **7.5 (3.7), 7 [2, 16]**; avg days between submissions 40.9 (25.0), 33 [4, 121] against a 2–4-week schedule.
  - Accelerometer data days included in analysis **113.7 (58.9), 108 [29, 267]**.
  - Avg accelerometer valid minutes/day over the whole observation period **558.7 (195.4), 577 [203, 944]**; within analysed days **724.7 (158.0), 743 [220, 972]** (max possible 1,440).
  - GPS valid data days in analysis **217.4 (86.4), 212 [52, 381]**.
  - Avg GPS collection minutes/day over observation period **68.6 (29.5), 68 [16, 145]**; within analysed days 87.7 (22.7), 83 [56, 153]. (Design maximum under 1-on/10-off is ~131 min/day; 68.6 is roughly half of design.)
  - "Phone on person" minutes/day baseline estimate **191.4** (of 728.2 valid minutes), i.e. the phone was in motion about a quarter of valid time (p.1384).
- **Definitions (p.1383, 1385):** valid accelerometer minute = "at least 20 sec (out of 30 sec expected) with at least 5 raw accelerometer data observations each (out of 10 expected)" (Fig.1 caption). Valid day = daily valid minutes within the participant's mean ±180 min and inside a 28-day window with ≥7 such days. GPS day = any GPS data and inside a 28-day window with ≥7 such days. Analytic inclusion = ≥2 complete ALSFRS-RSE, ≥28 valid accel days, ≥28 valid GPS days.
- **Stability over time (p.1384):** LMM monthly change in valid minutes/day = 1.01 (p=0.328) and in phone-on-person minutes = −0.568 (p=0.803): "no statistically significant change over time" in the analytic sample. (Note: this is conditioned on the analytic sample, which by construction has stable valid-minute counts.)
- **OS breakdown of completeness:** not reported.
- **Technical failure modes:** none itemised beyond the unlogged support calls and the by-design GPS gaps requiring imputation. Limitations name "some individuals may not always carry their smartphones with them" and "smartphone wear and usage variations could affect our measures in ALS patients with poor hand function" (p.1390).
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions (p.1388):** "The findings of this study demonstrate high feasibility and utility of remote monitoring through smartphone apps for both passive and active data collection in PALS." Recommends smartphones over wearables on inclusivity/cost grounds (p.1389–1390). Concedes "we lacked a well-established method to account for a varying number of valid accelerometer data minutes per day" (p.1390).
- **Not reported:** withdrawals/deaths; screened numbers; support-call volume; per-OS completeness; compensation; app-install failures (Johnson reported one); battery complaints (Johnson reported some); whether the 18 excluded stopped uploading or never met thresholds; heartbeat status (data collected 2021–2022, pre-heartbeat, so the Beukenhorst caveat applies).

- **File:** `2024-valeri-nppdigitalpsychiatryneur-smartphone-based-markers-social-connectivity-schizophrenia-bipolar.txt`
- **First author:** Linda Valeri (Columbia). Onnela and Baker co-senior.
- **Year / journal:** 2024, *NPP – Digital Psychiatry and Neuroscience*
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s44277-024-00013-w. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform actually deployed (Methods, p.2):** "Participants installed the Beiwe smartphone application on their personal smartphones… configured to send daily reminders at 5 pm ET… to fill a 31-item survey and to collect… mobile call and text logs." **Beiwe, BYOD, Android-only subset.** Matches filename.
- **Cohort:** Bipolar Longitudinal Study ("one hundred person-year study"), McLean Hospital, 74 enrolled 2015–2021. Likely the same programme as Kiang 2021's Study B ("McLean Hospital patients at risk for mania and psychosis"); Kiang analysed accelerometer/GPS metadata across it, so this paper's call/text-log subset is a different stream. No Module 3 profile covers it.
- **Verdict: REJECT, no-cohort. BORDERLINE-reject.** The analysed cohort is **9 of 74** enrolled, chosen because "Mobile communication activity was available for 10 patients enrolled between 2015 and 2019. A subset of 9 patients were followed for at least one month" (p.2). There is no recruitment funnel, no attrition, no reasons for the 64 without communication data beyond the implied iOS gate, and the paper is explicitly an n-of-1 feasibility analysis of a derived feature. The two operational facts worth keeping are (i) the structural gate: call/text logs were obtainable from only **10/74 (13.5%)** enrolees because "use of an Android phone made it possible for us to extract passive data on mobile communication" (p.2); and (ii) per-participant survey completeness ranged **19.4%–100% of days** (Table 1, p.4: 61.11, 26.36, 71.35, 71.48, 65.53, 36.05, 19.41, 26.09, 100%), with "no missing data in the sensor-based measures" (p.3). Both are already represented in the README's OS-gate finding (#23, Balliu 2024: SMS computable for 15/183). Recommend recording the 10/74 figure as a one-line corroboration in README #23 rather than a profile.

## 6. Weingarden 2025 — BORDERLINE-include (as the anchor for a single MetricWire + Beiwe BDD profile, with Weingarden 2026 folded in)

- **First author:** Hilary Weingarden (MGH). Onnela co-author.
- **Year / journal:** 2025, *Internet Interventions* 40:100833
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.invent.2025.100833. **PMCID:** not in PDF (PMID 40486130 appears in the 2026 paper's references).
- **Platform(s) actually deployed (Methods, p.2):** "The research coordinator helped eligible participants download two study apps, Metricwire (Metricwire, 2023) and Beiwe (Onnela et al., 2021), for collection of EMA and raw passive smartphone data, respectively." **MetricWire (Module 2 `metricwire`) for EMA; Beiwe (Module 2 `beiwe`) for GPS + accelerometer.** Matches filename. Trial registration NCT04254575.
- **Verdict: BORDERLINE-include.** Why include: it is a two-platform deployment (MetricWire EMA + Beiwe passive, each doing what it does best) in a nationally, fully remotely recruited clinical sample (BDD with suicide risk or substance use), with a stated enrolled→analysed step, a joint EMA-plus-sensor completeness figure, and the companion 2026 paper supplies the EMA compliance rate. Why borderline: this paper alone reports no compliance rate, no retention, no technical failure modes and no OS breakdown; on its own it is close to "N enrolled, N analysed". The two papers together clear the bar; neither does alone. Recommend one profile sourced from both.
- **Existing profile:** none (no BDD, MetricWire+Beiwe, or NCT04254575 in Module 3).

### Extraction (from 2025 unless marked "2026")

- **Design:** ~3-month naturalistic assessment; EMA in two 2-week waves (baseline, midpoint), passive sensing continuous for 3 months (p.2). Secondary-analysis framing in both papers; primary aims pre-registered.
- **Funding/COI:** NIMH K23MH119372 (Weingarden). Weingarden: HabitAware employment, Augmend Health and APA Labs advisory, Koa Health research funding; Armey: Ilumivu advisory (an EMA vendor, disclosed in 2026 as "could potentially benefit from the results"); Wilhelm: Koa Health, Noom, Jimini, One Mind. Onnela: no COI listed in either paper (2026: "JPO: none declared") despite the Beiwe/Phebe role declared elsewhere in this batch.
- **Population/setting/country:** US-based adults 18+ with primary BDD (MINI), BDD-YBOCS ≥24, plus past-month suicidal thoughts and/or past-2-week alcohol or marijuana use; excluded if active SI with intent or needing more than outpatient care. Analysed n=83: mean age 29.29 (SD 8.87); 74.7% White; 74.7% female; 100% ≥1 comorbidity; 59% on psychotropics (Table 1, p.3). Data collected **July 2020 – May 2023** (2026, p.5).
- **Enrolled → analysed (p.4, verbatim):** "The initial consented and eligible sample included 87 adults; after removing 4 participants who were missing passive smartphone data (n = 3) or had extreme outliers for passive smartphone data (n = 1), our final sample included 83." 2026 paper analyses all 87 "who completed any EMA questionnaires". So: **3/87 (3.4%) yielded no passive Beiwe data at all**; reason not stated. 2026 (p.11): **5 further individuals were excluded at screening** for active SI with plan/intent (C-SSRS item 1 ≥4) and referred out; screened total not reported.
- **Duration:** ~3 months; 28 EMA days (2 × 14) at 3 prompts/day = 84 prompts; passive 3 months.
- **Deployment model:** BYOD (Android or iOS required, plus Wi-Fi and a computer/tablet); fully virtual screening/baseline over HIPAA-compliant video; coordinator walked participants through app installation on a video call (2026, p.5). No compensation amounts reported in either paper.
- **Custom integration:** Forest library for daily summaries; sparse online Gaussian process GPS imputation; on-phone random offsets to GPS coordinates (p.3). Beiwe sampling: GPS 90 s every 15 min; accelerometer 10 Hz, 10 s every 30 s.
- **Recruitment:** national; clinic/program referrals, professional organisations, IOCDF newsletters, Facebook/Instagram/Reddit support groups and mental-health boards (p.2; 2026 p.5). Pre-screen survey → phone call with coordinator → virtual clinician screening.
- **Retention:** not reported as a rate in either paper.
- **Data completeness:**
  - **EMA compliance (2026, p.8, verbatim):** "out of 84 possible prompts, participants completed a mean of 61.25 (72%), SD = 16.21 (19%), and 80.46% of participants completed >60% of all EMA prompts." Prompt window 30 minutes. A morning daily diary (7–11 am) was also sent but not analysed.
  - **Joint EMA + sensor completeness (2025, p.4):** "we used the 85.9 % of participant days that had both ≥1 EMA entry and complete daily smartphone summary statistics." Also: "Missing data across smartphone summary statistics usually occurred simultaneously for a given participant, such that if one summary statistic was missing, some or all others were missing for that day" (a whole-day upload/collection failure signature rather than a per-sensor one).
  - Design note (2025, p.2): EMA split into two waves "to reduce burden and increase adherence".
- **Wear-time / OS breakdown:** not applicable / not reported.
- **Technical failure modes:** only the 3 participants with no passive data and the 1 outlier; nothing on app crashes, upload, battery. **Safety-protocol burden (2026, p.11):** telephone risk assessments were triggered by EMA suicide-desire score 10 or intent ≥8; number of triggers not reported.
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions:** 2026 (p.9) calls the EMA compliance "strong" and lists "strong EMA compliance over 84 prompts and 28 days" as a methodological strength. 2025 (p.6): "Due to the burden of collecting EMA 3 times daily, we collected emotion ratings for 28 days; future studies could collect EMA-rated outcomes fewer times per day over more days, to increase days with both sensor and EMA data." Also argues against adding wearables because they limit generalisability (p.6).
- **Not reported:** screened/approached numbers; compensation; withdrawals; per-wave compliance (wave 1 vs wave 2); OS split; why 3 participants produced no Beiwe data; support contacts; number of risk-protocol activations; passive-data completeness by day across the 3 months.

## 7. Nock 2026 — INCLUDE

- **File:** `2026-nock-jpsychopatholclinsci-smartphone-surveys-predict-next-week-suicide-attempts.txt`
- **First author:** Matthew K. Nock (Harvard). Onnela and Picard co-authors.
- **Year / journal:** 2026, *Journal of Psychopathology and Clinical Science* (HHS author manuscript, "available in PMC 2026 June 26")
- **DOI:** not printed in the author manuscript. **PMCID:** not in PDF.
- **Platform actually deployed (Method, p.3):** "Participants provided informed consent, completed a baseline survey, and installed an app on their smartphone (**LifeData**) that delivered brief self-report surveys." **LifeData (Module 2 profile `lifedata`), EMA-only; no passive sensing; not Beiwe.** The filename says only "smartphone surveys"; the platform is LifeData. Survey *metadata* (prompt-to-start lag, start-to-submit time, deferred submission, days since last survey, extreme-response count) was used as predictors.
- **Cohort relationships:** Not the Weingarden BDD cohort (different site, app, population, IRB-18-1749 vs 2019P002041). Not `beiwe-inpatient-suicide-pilot.md` (Wang 2021, N=104, 2016–2018, inpatient stay only, movisensXS/Beiwe), which this paper cites as prior work. Very likely the same cohort as Wang et al. 2024 *Nature Mental Health* (personalised models; same 20+ author list), which is not in Module 3.
- **Verdict: INCLUDE.** First LifeData deployment for Module 3; N=619, the largest EMA cohort in the module; explicit reporting that survey initiation ran below 50% and declined despite payment; a quantified enrolled→any-data→analysed funnel; and a clinically high-risk, two-site, adult+adolescent sample. It is also the only paper in this batch whose operational lesson (paid, 6×/day EMA in acutely suicidal patients still loses ~19% of enrolees before a single survey and runs at <50% initiation) is not already stated somewhere in Module 3 at this scale.

### Extraction (INCLUDE)

- **Design:** prospective 3-month EMA cohort with ML prediction of next-week suicide attempt/SRE; two recruitment sites (p.3).
- **Funding/COI (p.11):** NIMH U01MH116928, Will & Chet Griswold Fund, Fuss Family Research Fund. Nock: royalties, paid consulting (Cambridge Health Alliance, OpenAI, legal cases), stock options Cerebral, unpaid advisor to Empatica/Koko/TalkLife/JED; Picard: Empatica shareholder; Smoller: Sensorium, Data Driven, Tempus, Biogen. No LifeData relationship declared.
- **Population/setting/country:** 313 adults (18+) presenting to a psychiatric emergency service and 306 adolescents (12–19) presenting to a psychiatric inpatient unit, two Boston-area hospitals; inclusion required "owning an Android or iOS smartphone" (p.3). Analysed n=498: mean age 23.1 (SD 18.0); 74.5% White; 72.1% female at birth; 24.1% household income $0–20,999; 29.1% past-month suicide attempt (Table 1, p.18).
- **Enrolled → analysed (p.6, verbatim):** "Of the 619 unique participants consented, 502 (81.1%) provided data in at least one survey and those participants started a total of 79,448 surveys." "The final most inclusive sample… was 498 (246 adults, 252 adolescents) unique participants (80.5% of those recruited) who completed the affect questions on at least one survey. These participants most inclusively answered required questions on 77,592 surveys." Between 1,856 and 5,710 started surveys were "discontinued such that they became unusable" depending on feature set. So **117/619 (18.9%) consented but never submitted a survey.**
- **Duration:** EMA 6×/day for 3 months (84 days), then daily surveys for a further 3 months (not analysed) (p.3).
- **Deployment model:** BYOD (smartphone ownership was an inclusion criterion; no loaner phones, unlike Wang 2021). Compensation **$10 baseline + $1 per completed EMA** (p.3). Schedule: first and last prompt at participant-chosen fixed times, four middle prompts randomised ≥2 h apart; user-initiated event reports allowed; real-time risk monitoring on high-intent responses (p.4). Adolescents' notifications began "after discharge from the inpatient units" (p.4).
- **Custom integration:** EHR clinical-note review for outcome coding; no sensor integration.
- **Recruitment:** in-hospital consent at presentation; adults and adolescents "recruited differently" (limitation, p.10); screened/approached numbers not reported.
- **Retention definition and rate:** no formal retention rate. Proxy (p.6): "Over the 84 days… adult participants provided survey data for an average of **43 days (33.2 SD)** and adolescents for **40 days (25.7 SD)**." "rolling median and mean survey initalization rates were **<50%** over the three-month study period and **decreased over time** (see Figure S1)." (Figure S1 not in the manuscript text.)
- **Attrition reasons:** not reported.
- **Data completeness definition:** per-survey "started" vs "completed"; discontinued surveys excluded per feature set. Missingness was itself modelled: BiLSTM was chosen as a "missing-enabled" model and the authors note "BiLSTM incorporates information about missingness, whereas lasso does not" (p.11), echoing the Wang 2021 finding already in `beiwe-inpatient-suicide-pilot.md`. Metadata features "days since last survey on file" and "survey completed but not submitted until next prompt" were predictors (p.4); in the adult lasso, days-since-last-EMA OR=1.00 and delayed submission OR=1.05 (Table 2).
- **OS breakdown:** not reported.
- **Technical failure modes:** none reported (no app, notification or platform issues described).
- **Authors' feasibility conclusions (p.10–11, verbatim):** "there was a significant amount of missing data, even in the context of our paying participants for completing surveys, limiting the ecological validity of our findings and highlighting that the intensive longitudinal monitoring methods used will not be equally engaging to all participants. On balance, despite this level of missingness, we still had nearly 80,000 EMA datapoints… supporting the feasibility of this approach." They suggest briefer surveys may raise engagement since "much of our predictive power came from a smaller set of features" (p.11) and recommend adding passive physiological/behavioural and call/text data in future (p.11).
- **Not reported:** approached/eligible/declined counts; withdrawals; per-participant completion distribution; OS split; app/notification failures; per-site differences in engagement; whether the 117 zero-survey participants differed clinically; LifeData version or console; Figure S1 values.
