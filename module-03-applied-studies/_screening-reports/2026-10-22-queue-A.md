## 1. Kedoin et al. 2026, JMIR Formative Research

- Source read: Europe PMC full-text XML (PMC13673175), CC BY 4.0. No PDF obtainable (Europe PMC render returned HTML, PMC a bot check, JMIR PDF endpoint refused). XML-only.
- First author: Hayato Kedoin. The XML lists a JMIR editor (Sarvestan J) and reviewer (Kikuchi S) before the authors. Kedoin is first among `contrib-type="author"` records. Year 2026. Journal JMIR Form Res 10:e101615. DOI 10.2196/101615. PMCID PMC13673175 (not in a PDF; from the XML).
- Deployed (Methods, Study Flow): "wearable devices (Fitbit Inspire 3; Google LLC) were distributed, required apps were configured, and participants were enrolled in the study's LINE official account." Matches the queue guess (Fitbit).
- Verdict: INCLUDE. Technical and operational feasibility is a stated aim, with defined valid-night rule, per-arm wear medians, delivery and questionnaire completion, and a described custom integration. Thin because N=25 with 100% retention and no reported failure modes.
- Existing profile on this cohort: none.

Extraction
- Design: single-team stratified 1:1 pilot RCT; retrospectively registered UMIN000061189 on 2026-04-07 (Methods, Ethics).
- Funding/COI: 2025 Nippon Sport Science University Academic Research Grant. No funding, equipment or support from Fitbit, Google, OpenAI or LY Corporation. Authors built the prototype. Monetary compensation paid, amount not reported (Ethical Considerations).
- Population: 25 female players, one high school team, Japan; mean age 16.93 (SD 0.70); experience 9.61 y (SD 2.14) (Participants).
- Enrolled to analysed: 25 to 25; 12 intervention, 13 control; no withdrawals; no exclusions after screening (Results; Eligibility).
- Duration: setup 2025-08-18; baseline from 2025-09-01; final assessment 2025-11-03; weeks 0 to 10 (Study Flow).
- Deployment model: provisioned Fitbit, collected at week 10; own smartphone required for LINE and Fitbit sync.
- Custom integration: Google Apps Script, Fitbit Web API `/1.2/user/-/sleep/date/{date}.json`, OAuth 2.0 with refresh-token retry, `isMainSleep` filter, Google Sheets, OpenAI Responses API (GPT-4o), LINE Messaging API, dedup flags, exponential backoff, pre-trial dummy testing (Intervention section).
- Recruitment: all eligible available team members; number approached not reported in text.
- Retention: 100%. Questionnaire completion 100% at baseline and post.
- Completeness: valid wear night = main sleep episode with all required variables; no minimum nights; no imputation. Median valid nights 26 (IQR 24 to 28) intervention, 22 (IQR 15 to 26) control (Technical and Operational Feasibility). Window and denominator not stated.
- Technical: 100% LINE delivery of messages sent; "No major technical problems"; no adverse events; no privacy breaches.
- Authors' conclusions: technical and operational feasibility in a single-team setting; engagement and acceptability not assessed; recommend prospective registration, larger samples, cluster randomization by team.
- Not reported: wear-night window, days without a message, token refresh or retry counts, OS breakdown, battery/charging, device loss, compensation amount, message reading, acceptability.

## 2. Tan et al. 2026, Frontiers in Physiology

- Source read: publisher PDF (16 pages, CC BY). Stored at `literature/2026-tan-frontphysiol-proudme-summer-obesity-prevention-fitbit-actigraph.pdf` (magic bytes `%PDF-`, text extraction works).
- First author (PDF byline): Qiaoyin Tan. Year 2026. Journal Front Physiol 17:1914161. DOI 10.3389/fphys.2026.1914161. PMCID: not in PDF (PMC13623721 per Europe PMC).
- Deployed (Methods 2.3.2): "Participants were also provided with Fitbit Charge 6 devices to support PA awareness and self-monitoring throughout the ProudMe Summer intervention. ActiGraph GT3X+ accelerometers were used in two ways." Matches the queue guess (Fitbit + ActiGraph).
- Verdict: INCLUDE. Usage of the technology components is a stated aim, and the paper reports device loss, sync failures, usable-record counts and the spread of recorded days.
- Existing profile on this cohort: none.

Extraction
- Design: non-randomized pilot controlled trial, allocation by availability, not prospectively registered; LSU IRB IRBAM-25-0015 (2.1).
- Funding/COI: Louisiana Board of Regents LEQSF(2022-24)-RD-A-03; LSU Foundation/Our Lady of the Lake Collaboration in Action 2024; Pliner Professorship. No conflicts declared. Compensation not reported.
- Population: ages 11 to 14, within 10 miles of LSU, moderate to high ADI; 17 M / 9 F; 13 Black, 9 Asian, 3 White, 1 Hispanic (Table 2).
- Enrolled to analysed: 26 baseline (IG 14, CG 12); 1 IG withdrew after week 1; 25 post. Abstract says 25 participants. Fitbit usable 10 of 13 IG. ActiGraph valid pre IG 14 / CG 12, post IG 13 / CG 10 (Figure 7 legend). Camp-hour ActiGraph n=13.
- Duration: 8 weeks, summer 2025.
- Deployment model: Fitbit provisioned; app on participant's own device (eligibility, access 15 min/day).
- Custom integration: ProudMe Tech app (React.js, Node.js/Express, MongoDB, GPT-4o API, 75 tokens, temperature 0.9, OpenAI Moderation API). Not linked to Fitbit. Data downloaded to a local drive.
- Recruitment: parent-facing flyers and social media; online registration and screening form. Screened count not in text.
- Completeness: Fitbit reported day = any exported day with non-zero activity; no wear-time threshold. Range 4 to 43 days; 196 person-days; 19.6 (SD 14.2) per participant; 30,079 min; 153.5 min per reported day (Table 4). ActiGraph valid day at least 10 h (2.6).
- Technical failures: "one lost Fitbit, and two participants had unsuccessful device synchronization, resulting in no exportable activity data" (3.3.2).
- App: 851 entries; 271 week 1, 247 week 2, then steady decline (3.3.3). Acceptability 54.4% to 58.3% (Table 3).
- Authors' conclusions: components implementable in the structured camp; sustained use post-camp limited; recommend automated reminders, missed-entry notifications, check-ins, parental integration, longer camp exposure.
- Not reported: withdrawal reason, sync failure cause, device replacement, Fitbit export route, OS breakdown, battery, Fitbit days by phase.

## 3. Cook et al. 2026, AIDS and Behavior

- Source read: medRxiv preprint 10.64898/2026.03.29.26349681, posted 2026-03-31, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Unpaywall and OpenAlex list it as the green OA copy. The published version is paywalled and was not read. Not stored, since the verdict is reject and the file is a preprint rather than the version of record.
- First author (preprint byline): Paul F. Cook. Year 2026. DOI 10.1007/s10461-026-05317-w. PMCID: not in PDF.
- Deployed (Method, Primary Outcome): "Participants wore an ActiGraph GT3X monitor (ActiGraph LLC, Fort Walton Beach FL) on their non-dominant hip for four 7-day periods, at study weeks 16, 20, 24, and 28." Matches the queue guess (ActiGraph). Messages ran on a Caspio-built SMS and email system, not a Module 2 platform.
- Verdict: BORDERLINE-reject, no-cohort. ActiGraph is the outcome instrument of a messaging trial, and the deployment content is a CONSORT flow plus valid-actigraphy counts at analysis (23 of 46 and 26 of 46). There is no wear time, no per-wave completeness narrative and no technical failure content beyond "substantial missing data".
- Existing profile on this cohort: none (HEALTH study, NCT04550676).
- Numbers that would matter if reconsidered: 212 assessed, 142 consented, 118 randomized, 92 re-randomized; phase 2 discontinued as nonadherent 11 and 13, lost to follow-up 7 and 5; valid-actigraphy rule at least 4 days with 10 h including a weekend day (Fig. 1; Method).
