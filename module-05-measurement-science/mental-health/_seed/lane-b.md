# Module 5, Mental health: Lane B seed (measurement science)

Prepared 2026-10-23. Lane B covers the measurement science of mental-health constructs measured with
smartphones and wearables: feature definitions (GPS mobility, rest-activity, phone use, sociability,
speech and voice, keyboard dynamics), device-agnostic methods and open tools (feature pipelines,
benchmark datasets, personalised versus population models), pooled evidence (systematic reviews and
meta-analyses of associations and prediction performance for depression, anxiety, stress, bipolar
disorder and psychosis), and construct-validity limits (overfitting, cross-cohort generalisation,
device and operating-system differences). Guidance, consensus, validation frameworks, EMA item
measurement and reporting are left to Lane A.

31 papers kept: 9 PDFs stored and verified, 14 open access but not obtained this pass, 5 free to read
but not stored for licence reasons, 3 paywalled.

## 5. Gaps

- No paper found that reports test-retest reliability (for example ICCs across weeks) of GPS, phone-use
  or sociability features as such; reliability appears only indirectly (H-B04, H-B05). A targeted
  search on behavioural stability or trait-like sensing in personality science may fill it.
- Nothing specific on confounding of mental-health features by weather, season or daylight, or on
  measurement reactivity to passive sensing, turned up in Europe PMC; ACM and IEEE venues (IMWUT,
  UbiComp) are under-indexed there and should be searched through Crossref or DBLP next.
- Rest-activity and circadian features (relative amplitude, interdaily stability) in mental health are
  covered only by one actigraphy meta-analysis (H-B21); check the Sleep section and Lane A before
  adding more, since the definitions largely live in sleep research.
