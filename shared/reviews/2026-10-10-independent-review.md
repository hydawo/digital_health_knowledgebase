### How trustworthy is it?

The literature side is solid. Papers are almost always attributed to the right study and the right platform. Every one of the 12 sampled Module 3 profiles names the correct device or platform. Their headline enrolment and retention figures match the full text, and every DOI checked against Crossref points to the right paper. The work added in the last two days is also mostly accurate: about 80% of changed claims check out.

The weakness is on the vendor side, and it is the part that drives study decisions. The comparison matrices for Module 1 and Module 2 were last rebuilt on 2026-08-24 and have fallen behind both the profiles and the vendors. Only about half of the sampled matrix cells could be confirmed. Several errors would send a study team the wrong way:

- The Fitbit legacy API is shown as usable; it is turned off on 2026-10-30, and Google is not onboarding new Google Health API projects.
- Garmin's developer program is shown as open within two business days; access requests are reported paused (Corroborated, not on an official Garmin page).
- Samsung's raw EDA, PPG and ECG streams are said not to exist or to be undocumented.
- WHOOP's ECG clearance and its blood-pressure warning letter are reversed.
- Withings, MetricWire and Oura publish compliance statements the knowledge base says it could not find.
- Program-level access terms are missing entirely: WHOOP's member tiers and AI-training ban, Oura's no-retention terms, and Withings' paid-membership change from 2026-10-12.

The research-program layer is the weakest part: 97 of the 125 program claims are wrong, unsupported, overstated, unverifiable, in conflict or missing. Internal consistency is the second weakness: the explorer, the atlas, the module READMEs and the matrices often disagree with the profiles they summarise.

In short, trust the study profiles and the bibliography. Re-verify any vendor access, pricing or compliance statement against the vendor before acting on it, and treat the comparison matrices as stale until they are rebuilt from the profiles.

### Most consequential issues (consolidated across checks)

1. **Fitbit / Google** (R-003, R-016 to R-019, R-027):
   - Legacy Fitbit Web API support ended 2026-09-30, and the API is turned off on 2026-10-30. Google's page says so, read directly on 2026-10-10.
   - Every Google Health API page says Google is "not onboarding new projects at this time".
   - The knowledge base says the API turns down in September and that the Google Health API is open to new integrations.
2. **WHOOP regulatory status** (R-002, R-004):
   - The ECG feature is FDA 510(k) K243236, cleared 2025-04-04 (confirmed in openFDA).
   - Blood Pressure Insights is not cleared. FDA issued a warning letter dated 2025-07-14; check Q2 reports it was closed on 2026-06-17 under enforcement discretion.
3. **Samsung raw signals** (R-005 and related):
   - The Samsung Health Sensor SDK documents raw EDA at 1 Hz on Galaxy Watch8 and later, PPG at 25 Hz continuous and 100 Hz on demand, and ECG at 500 Hz.
   - This means "Empatica is the only raw EDA source" is wrong.
4. **Garmin** (R-020 to R-022): Garmin Connect Developer Program access requests are reported paused since spring 2026 (Corroborated: a Garmin developer-forum thread and two secondary sources). Enhanced BBI carries a licence fee for commercial use.
5. **Access terms the knowledge base omits** (R-024, R-028 to R-031):
   - **WHOOP** (terms effective 2026-10-06): member tiers starting at 10 users, a ban on permanent copies, and a ban on ML/AI training.
   - **Oura** (API Agreement effective 2026-06-08): no retention beyond what is strictly necessary, and 72-hour deletion on request.
   - **Withings:** a free self-serve plan, with a paid Withings+ membership required for accounts created from 2026-10-12.
6. **Compliance statements missed:**
   - **Withings** publishes HIPAA, GDPR, ISO 27001 and HDS statements (R-006).
   - **MetricWire** states ISO 27001, SOC II Type II, 21 CFR Part 11, HIPAA and GDPR (R-013). These are vendor statements without linked certificates, and metricwire.com is no longer blocked.
   - **Avicenna's ISO 27001** is presented as Verified and "independently audited", but the only source is the vendor's own page (R-012).
7. **Apple SensorKit** offers photoplethysmogram, electrocardiogram and accelerometer sensors to approved studies. Confirmed in Apple's SRSensor documentation. The knowledge base says Apple offers no raw PPG by any route (R-025).
8. **Oura** has a first-party "Researchers" offering under Oura for Organizations, plus an Oura Research app, which the knowledge base says does not exist (R-023). Personal access tokens are retired, and the 5,000 requests per 5 minutes figure is no longer published.
9. **Module 2 platform facts that change a selection:**
   - **mindLAMP** is not open source end to end: the server and apps have no licence file. It does have a supported hosted pathway, mindLAMP Core (R-010, R-026).
   - **AWARE** is on the App Store (R-011, R-015).
   - **RADAR-base's** iOS passive app is a dormant work in progress that records location only (R-009).
   - **Beiwe's** Android-only streams are not labelled in the matrix (R-007, R-008).
   - **"Only Beiwe offers paid hosting"** is no longer true: mindLAMP, CARP and RADAR-base hosting all exist.
10. **Module 3 figures:**
    - Spangenberg 2026 phase-2 compliance is 45.4% in the paper but 47.4% in the feasibility matrix and explorer (R-014).
    - Matcham 2022's 17.7% figure is the share of participants with more than 50% data across all streams, not "cross-stream completeness" (R-071).
    - Several funding rows list IRBs or affiliations instead of funders.
