# Module 2 — Literature Library

Companion to `sources.md`. Where `sources.md` records *all* sources used to build this module
(vendor docs, GitHub repos, product pages, news items, and academic papers alike), this file is
narrower and deeper: it catalogs **only genuine academic literature** — peer-reviewed journal
articles, conference papers, and preprints — cited across the Module 2 profiles, plus a handful of
additional decision-relevant papers surfaced by a fresh literature search per platform. For open-access
papers, the actual PDF is stored locally under `literature/<platform-slug>/` rather than just linked,
per this project's literature-library convention (see `shared/research-log.md`, "2026-08-24 (later) —
Literature-library scope decision").

**Scope note:** this is not an exhaustive bibliography. Per `CLAUDE.md`'s evidence standard, it
targets "the strongest and most decision-relevant evidence" per platform — typically one methods/
platform-description paper plus one or two major validation/deployment/comparison studies — not
every paper that ever mentions a platform.

**Unlike Module 1's `research-library-wearables.md`**, this file does not use a funding/COI tier
system. These are software-platform papers (academic groups describing their own open-source or
commercial tooling), not device-validation studies with vendor-funded accuracy claims, so the tiering
convention doesn't transfer cleanly. Where a paper's authors are the platform's own developers, that
is noted in the Relevance column instead.

**OA-status confidence markers**, matching this knowledge base's evidence-confidence standard:
- **Verified** — the actual PDF was fetched and inspected directly (magic-byte + text-content check),
  and/or an explicit CC BY / open-access license statement was read directly on the article page or in
  the PDF itself.
- **Corroborated** — multiple secondary sources (e.g., PubMed, journal listing pages) agree the venue
  is open-access (e.g., JMIR's blanket CC BY policy, arXiv's inherent openness), but the specific
  article's license statement was not directly read.
- **Unclear** — access status could not be established from available evidence.

All entries below are **Verified** unless noted otherwise — every PDF in `literature/` was downloaded
and confirmed to be a genuine, readable PDF (not an HTML paywall/CAPTCHA page) before being kept.

**Last verified: 2026-08-24.**

# Onnela Lab publication catalog — "Digital Health & Phenotyping"

**Added 2026-08-31.** Source: the Onnela Lab (Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health) publications
page, <https://hsph.harvard.edu/research/onnela-lab/papers/>, fetched directly (HTTP 200) on
2026-08-31. That page groups its output under exactly two `<h2>` headings — **"Digital Health and
Phenotyping"** and **"Network Science"**. Only the first was pulled; the network-science half is out
of scope for this knowledge base. The category structure was unambiguous, so nothing was pulled on a
judgment call about which heading a paper sat under.

**The page listed 114 entries under that heading; 113 are distinct papers** (one paper — Hu et al.,
*Nature and Science of Sleep* 2025, on wrist-worn consumer-wearable vs. actigraphy sleep measurement —
is listed twice on the page). This section catalogs the **91 that belong to Module 2**; the
remaining 22 are wearable-primary or Apple-ecosystem papers and are catalogued in
[`../module-01-wearables/literature-library.md`](../module-01-wearables/literature-library.md).

**Scope difference from the rest of this file.** Everything above this line is a *curated* selection —
"the strongest and most decision-relevant evidence" per platform. This section is different in kind:
it is a **complete catalog pull** of one lab's digital-health output, captured because the Onnela Lab
is Beiwe's and Forest's originator and its publication record is the primary evidence base for both.
It is deliberately exhaustive rather than selective, and the two conventions coexist rather than one
replacing the other.

**Two-layer filing rule.** Every paper here has exactly one catalog home, determined by the *platform*
it ran on (Beiwe / smartphone phenotyping → Module 2; wearable or Apple ecosystem → Module 1). A
subset are also **Module 3 candidates** — real research deployments that report how the deployment
went (recruitment, retention, adherence, wear time, data completeness, technical failure modes).
Those carry a forward pointer in the "What it establishes" column; the operational detail itself lives
in Module 3, not duplicated here. Pure methods and architecture papers (GPS imputation, activity
recognition, platform description) stay catalog-only.

**Folder reorganisation, 2026-09-03.** The `literature/onnela-lab/` folder was pulled on the assumption
that the lab's digital-health output would all be Beiwe work. Full-text screening showed it was not, so
the folder was dissolved and each paper filed by what it actually used. Beiwe deployments and Beiwe
analytics papers moved to `literature/beiwe/` (35). Two LifeData deployments (Nock 2026, Coppersmith
2024) moved to `literature/lifedata/`. Liu 2019 used the LAMP app and moved to `literature/mindlamp/`.
Torous 2015 used a one-off app called Mindful Moods and Lee 2024 a contractor-built vaping study app;
each now has its own folder (`mindful-moods/`, `vaping-health-study-app/`) as a placeholder for a
future profile. Ten reviews and position papers moved to
[`../module-04-methods-and-reviews/`](../module-04-methods-and-reviews/README.md). Six pure statistics
papers with no digital-health platform were deleted from the library and are marked below.

**Evidence discipline.** Adding a paper to this catalog does **not** upgrade any existing claim's
confidence status anywhere else in the knowledge base. Abstracts below are Europe PMC's, quoted only
far enough to characterize what each paper establishes; no claim in any profile has been re-labelled
on the strength of this pass.

**OA status column** uses the same vocabulary as the rest of this file, with one addition:
- **Verified OA** — the PDF was downloaded and confirmed genuine (magic-byte + `pypdf` text-extraction
  check on the first three pages), stored under `literature/beiwe/` (the `onnela-lab/` folder was dissolved on 2026-09-03; see the note below).
- **OA, not obtained** — Europe PMC's own metadata marks the record `isOpenAccess: Y`, but neither the
  Europe PMC render service nor the PMC direct-PDF route returned a PDF (HTTP 500 / 403 / HTML page).
  This is an infrastructure failure, not a paywall, and is recorded as such.
- **Unclear** — a PMC author-manuscript deposit exists but is not flagged open access; no PDF obtained.
- **Paywalled** — no open-access copy located: publisher paywall, no PMC deposit, no preprint.
- **Not indexed** — the lab page lists the item as *submitted*, *in press*, or preprint-only, with no
  DOI and no indexed record. These are recorded rather than dropped, because the lab page is itself the
  primary source for their existence.

**Metadata provenance.** Title, author list, venue and year were taken from the lab page; DOI, PMID,
PMCID, journal name, OA flag and abstract were resolved against Europe PMC by exact-title, then
DOI, then fuzzy-title lookup. Where the lab page's title and the Europe PMC record's title diverged
materially, the divergence is noted in the row rather than silently reconciled.

### Papers not obtained as PDFs, and why

Of the 91 Module 2 papers, **54 were obtained as verified open-access PDFs** and 2 were already in the
knowledge base (the 2016 Torous *JMIR Mental Health* Beiwe paper and the 2021 Onnela *JOSS* Beiwe
software paper, both already under `literature/beiwe/` — these were **not** re-downloaded or
duplicated). The remaining 35 are flagged individually in the PDF column above. The failure modes,
grouped:

- **Paywalled, no OA route** — publisher-only titles, chiefly *Annals of Surgery*, *Neurosurgery*,
  *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, *Psychiatry Research*, *Annual Review of Clinical Psychology*,
  *Statistical Methods in Medical Research*, *Biometrics*, *IEEE/ACM TASLP*, *Psychiatric Annals*,
  and *The American Journal of Bioethics*.
- **OA per Europe PMC but unretrievable this pass** — Europe PMC's render service returned HTTP 500
  repeatedly for a cluster of older PMC author manuscripts, and `pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/.../pdf/`
  returned HTML rather than a PDF for the JAMA-family articles. Recorded as an infrastructure
  barrier, not a licensing one; worth retrying in a later pass.
- **Not indexed** — items the lab page itself labels *Submitted* or *In print* with no DOI
  (e.g. the data-missingness clinical-inference paper, the year-long voice-diary NLP paper, the 2017
  mood-disorder app systematic review). Nothing exists to download yet.

No paper was silently omitted: every one of the 114 listed entries is either in this table, in Module
1's table, or is the single duplicate listing noted above.
